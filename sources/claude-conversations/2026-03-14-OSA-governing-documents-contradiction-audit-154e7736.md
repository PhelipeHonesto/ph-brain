### CONTRADICTION 1 — Charter Commission Rate (Three-Way Conflict)

**Conflicting documents:** Roadmap v3, Execution Memo v5, and OSA Connect Operating System

**Roadmap v3 (line 254):**
> "Commission on charter bookings, typically 5–10% of gross charter price"

**Roadmap v3 (line 713–714):**
> "Charter commissions are 5–10% of bookings."

**Execution Memo v5 (line 1185):**
> "Charter commissions are modeled at 7% of gross bookings."

**OSA Connect Operating System (line 266–267):**
> "Broker commission structure confirmed (typically 10–15% of charter rate, sometimes flat fee)"

**Nature of conflict:** The Roadmap says 5–10%. The Memo models at 7% (within that range). The Operating System says 10–15%. The Operating System's range barely overlaps with the Roadmap's range and doesn't overlap at all with the Memo's 7% modeling assumption. This is a material contradiction — the number the founder uses to vet operators (10–15%) is significantly higher than the number used to project revenue (7%) and the range described at the strategic level (5–10%).

**Recommended authority:** The Roadmap's 5–10% range is accurate for what brokers *typically earn* from operators. The Operating System's 10–15% appears to describe what operators *charge as their standard broker commission structure* — which is the gross commission the operator builds into the charter rate, from which the broker's actual net commission is derived. These may be describing different sides of the same transaction, but the language doesn't make this clear. The Memo's 7% is the planning model and sits within the Roadmap range.

**Recommended fix:** In the Operating System, Tool 3, Section D, change "Broker commission structure confirmed (typically 10–15% of charter rate, sometimes flat fee)" to "Broker commission structure confirmed and documented (industry range varies; see Execution Memo financial assumptions for OSA planning baseline)." This removes the conflicting number from the operational tool and points back to the governing financial model. Alternatively, if 10–15% is accurate for *operator-side* commission structures, add a clarifying note: "Note: operator-stated commission rates may differ from OSA's realized commission rate used in financial planning (see Execution Memo Section 7)."

### CONTRADICTION 2 — Stale Cadence Reference in Execution Memo (Residual from Pre-v1.2)

**Conflicting documents:** Execution Memo v5 vs. Content Strategy v1.2

**Execution Memo v5, Section 9, Phase 2 Operating Strategy (line 1519–1520):**
> "Newsletter: weekly if sustainable."

**Content Strategy v1.2 (line 41):**
> Newsletter cadence is locked at **biweekly**.

**Execution Memo v5, Section 9, Phase 1 Operating Strategy (line 1494–1496):**
> "Locked cadence: LinkedIn 4/week, Instagram 4/week, newsletter biweekly" — this is correct and aligned.

**Nature of conflict:** The Phase 1 cadence in Section 9 is correctly aligned with v1.2. But the Phase 2 cadence guidance in the same section says the newsletter should move to "weekly if sustainable." Content Strategy v1.2 locks the cadence at biweekly with no mention of a Phase 2 weekly upgrade. The Memo's own content doctrine handoff note (lines 1441–1448) says "all content cadence… governed exclusively by v1.2" and that "v1.2 supersedes any cadence guidance in prior versions of this memo." However, this Phase 2 weekly newsletter guidance survived into v5 itself — making it internally contradictory within the Memo, not just cross-document.

**Recommended authority:** Content Strategy v1.2 governs cadence. The Phase 2 weekly newsletter language in Section 9 is aspirational strategic guidance for a future state, but it conflicts with the Memo's own handoff declaration. 

**Recommended fix:** In Execution Memo v5, Section 9, Phase 2 Operating Strategy, change "Newsletter: weekly if sustainable" to "Newsletter: continue biweekly per v1.2. Weekly cadence evaluated only when v1.2 is formally updated based on audience data and capacity." This preserves the aspiration while respecting the locked doctrine.

### CONTRADICTION 3 — "Brief Take" Frequency vs. Locked LinkedIn Cadence

**Conflicting documents:** Execution Memo v5 vs. Content Strategy v1.2

**Execution Memo v5, Section 14, Recurring Content Formats (line 2071–2073):**
> "Brief Take: 100–150 word LinkedIn post with a clear opinion. Frequency: 2–3 per week on LinkedIn."

**Content Strategy v1.2 (line 34):**
> Total LinkedIn target cadence is 4 posts per week across all types (backward-tied, forward-testing, real-time signal).

**Nature of conflict:** If Brief Takes alone are 2–3 per week, and total LinkedIn posts are 4 per week, that means Brief Takes consume 50–75% of all LinkedIn output. Content Strategy v1.2 doesn't define "Brief Take" as a format at all — it organizes LinkedIn content by content type (backward-tied, forward-testing, real-time signal) and by editorial lane (TG, MS, HIAW, SA). The Memo's format taxonomy and the ContentStrat's content-type taxonomy don't map to each other, which could create operational confusion about what "counts" toward the 4/week cadence.

**Recommended authority:** Content Strategy v1.2 governs format roles and content types on LinkedIn. The Memo's "Brief Take" format is compatible but its stated frequency (2–3/week) is format-level guidance that v1.2 doesn't use. This isn't a hard contradiction — it's a taxonomy mismatch that could cause confusion.

**Recommended fix:** In Execution Memo v5, Section 14, add a parenthetical: "Brief Take: 100–150 word LinkedIn post with a clear opinion. Frequency: 2–3 per week on LinkedIn *(within the 4/week locked cadence governed by Content Strategy v1.2; Brief Takes map to forward-testing and real-time signal content types)*." This bridges the two taxonomies.

### CONTRADICTION 4 — Revenue Range Discrepancy Between Roadmap and Memo (Year 1)

**Conflicting documents:** Roadmap v3 vs. Execution Memo v5

**Roadmap v3, Phase 1 Revenue Table (line 690):**
> "Year 1: OSA Connect (Gross Bookings) $0–$100K"

**Execution Memo v5, Scenario A (lines 1217):**
> "Year 1 Total: $0–$120K"

**Execution Memo v5, Scenario B (line 1252):**
> "Year 1 Total: $30K–$230K"

**Nature of conflict:** The Roadmap says Year 1 gross bookings top out at $100K. The Memo's *conservative* scenario (Scenario A, the planning baseline) has Year 1 topping out at $120K. The Roadmap and the Memo's Scenario A should be identical since both claim to be the conservative planning baseline, but there's a $20K gap at the top of the range. This is minor but technically a discrepancy between the two documents that claim to use the same conservative assumptions.

**Recommended authority:** The Execution Memo v5 is the operating layer with the more detailed financial model. Its $0–$120K Scenario A number should be treated as authoritative.

**Recommended fix:** In Roadmap v3, Phase 1 Revenue Table, Year 1, update "$0–$100K" to "$0–$120K" to match the Memo's Scenario A. Or add a note that the Roadmap uses rounded figures and the Memo contains the detailed model.
