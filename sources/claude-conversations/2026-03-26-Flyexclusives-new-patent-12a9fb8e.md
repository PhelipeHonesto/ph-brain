Great, I have comprehensive info on this. Let me summarize it for Phelipe.Just announced yesterday at NBAA SDC 2026 in Cleveland — flyExclusive filed a utility patent application (App. Ser. No. 19/568,916) titled **"Aircraft Schedule Optimization."**

The patent describes a graph-based architecture that fundamentally rethinks how fleets handle scheduling and disruption recovery. Instead of recalculating schedules from scratch when conditions change, the system precomputes valid options across the entire fleet so that when disruptions occur, the answer is already available.

They announced this alongside a second milestone: the launch of **Contrails**, their new Flight Management System, which will be available to other Part 135 operators starting in Q2 2026. Contrails was built by combining flyExclusive's operational experience with IP acquired from Volato Group, including its Mission Control flight management system, which flyExclusive purchased for approximately $1.3 million in Class A common stock.

The interesting strategic play here: they're offering Contrails to other operators at no license cost. CEO Jim Segrave has been clear that the value isn't in selling scheduling software — it's in improving network efficiency by enabling operators to securely share aircraft availability without compromising customer identities or proprietary data.

This is a pretty sharp move from a competitive intelligence standpoint, Phelipe. They're essentially trying to become the infrastructure layer for Part 135 scheduling, which gives them visibility into fleet availability across the industry while strengthening their own wholesale charter network. Worth keeping an eye on for Loud Hawk coverage and for how it might affect OSA Connect's operator relationships down the line.

**The patent (graph-based schedule optimization):**

Think of it like this. Today, most Part 135 operators run scheduling the way you'd expect — a dispatcher has a fleet of aircraft, a set of trips, crew duty limits, and maintenance windows. When something breaks (weather delay, mechanical, crew timeout), they essentially have to re-solve the puzzle manually or with basic tools. It's reactive.

What flyExclusive is patenting is a system that pre-builds a massive decision tree (a "graph") of every valid schedule configuration across the fleet *before* disruptions happen. So when a CJ3 goes mechanical in Teterboro, the system doesn't start from scratch — it already has pre-computed fallback schedules ready to go. It just picks the best one. The speed advantage at scale is significant when you're running 100+ aircraft.

**Contrails (the free FMS):**

This is the strategic chess move. By giving away their flight management system to other operators for free, flyExclusive is doing two things:

First, they get network visibility. If operators across the industry are running on their platform, flyExclusive can see available lift in real time — making their wholesale charter sourcing faster and smarter without cold-calling operators.

Second, they create switching costs. Once an operator builds their workflows around Contrails, moving off it is painful. It's the classic platform play — give the tool away, monetize the network.

**What it means for the brokerage world:**

For someone in your position building OSA Connect, this is worth watching closely. If flyExclusive succeeds in getting broad operator adoption, they're essentially building the connective tissue between operators — the exact layer a broker sits in. It could make sourcing lift easier (more transparency), or it could cut brokers out if flyExclusive uses that network data to go direct. Their language about "not sharing customer identities" suggests they're aware operators are nervous about exactly that.

The short version: flyExclusive is trying to become the operating system for Part 135 scheduling, not just a charter company. That's a fundamentally different competitive position than running jets.

Traditional scheduling works like a spreadsheet. You have aircraft, trips, crews, maintenance windows, and a dispatcher solving the puzzle. When something breaks, you're re-solving from scratch — calling around, moving pieces manually.

A graph-based approach models every aircraft, every airport, every crew pairing, every maintenance window as nodes in a network, with edges representing valid transitions between states. Think of it like a massive pre-built decision tree. Before any disruption happens, the system has already mapped out: "If N123EX goes mechanical at TEB, here are the 14 valid schedule configurations that keep everything else legal and on time." The system just picks the best pre-computed path instead of rebuilding from zero.

The key claim seems to be that the performance advantage scales with fleet size. For a 10-aircraft operator, the brute force approach works fine. For flyExclusive running 100+ aircraft, the combinatorial complexity explodes. That's where the graph pre-computation gives you a real edge.

That's 300 daily trips they're leaving on the table. The patent isn't just about operational efficiency — it's about unlocking revenue they're currently losing because their scheduling can't keep up with demand. That's the real business case behind the filing.
