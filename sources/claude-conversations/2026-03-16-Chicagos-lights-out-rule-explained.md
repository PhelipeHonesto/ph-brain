Chicago's "Lights Out" isn't a brand-new rule — it's a long-running voluntary program, but it's very much active right now since spring migration season just kicked off on March 15.

Here's the gist: During migratory season, millions of birds representing hundreds of species fly through Chicago on their way to their summer or winter homes, and lights from tall buildings can disrupt these migratory paths, causing birds to circle buildings repeatedly — many die from exhaustion or after colliding with buildings.

The Lights Out program asks building owners and managers to reduce the total light emitted from buildings from 11 PM until sunrise during migratory seasons — mid-March to early June for spring migration, and late August to mid-November for fall migration. That means dimming or killing exterior decorative lighting (spotlights, logos, lighted clock faces, antenna lights), dimming lobby lights, and minimizing perimeter room lighting.

Chicago's Lights Out program launched in 2000 and was the first of its kind in the nation. Since 1995, participating buildings in the Loop have saved an estimated 10,000 birds' lives annually.

On the legislative side, there's been a related development at the state level: the Illinois Outdoor Lighting Control Act (SB3501), signed by Governor Pritzker, took effect January 1, 2025, and is one of the first state statutes in the country to address light pollution, particularly from modern outdoor LED lighting. That law currently governs properties managed by the Illinois Department of Natural Resources.

So if you're seeing "Lights Out" talk right now, it's likely because we're right at the start of spring migration season (March 15 – June 15). It's still primarily voluntary for private buildings, though there's been ongoing advocacy to make it a city ordinance.
