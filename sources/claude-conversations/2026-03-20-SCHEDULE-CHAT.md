## The OSA Day System

**Two day types. That's it.**

**BUILD days** — You have 3+ hours of uninterrupted time. These are for Claude Code sessions, writing Loud Hawk editions, and anything that requires deep focus. One primary task. One backup task if you finish early or get blocked.

**OPS days** — You have fragmented time (between flights, short gaps, hotel rooms, commute). These are for emails, operator outreach, LLC paperwork, CRM updates, Obsidian processing, LinkedIn posts. Small tasks that move the business forward without requiring flow state.

You decide which type each day is **the night before**, based on tomorrow's schedule. Not in the morning. Not when you sit down. The night before.

## The Nightly 3-Line Check-in

Every night, before you close your laptop, you write three lines in Obsidian:

```
Day type: BUILD / OPS
Top 1: [the one thing that matters tomorrow]
If done early: [backup task]
```

That's the entire system. When you wake up and sit down, you don't decide. You read your three lines and start.

## The Hyperfocus Guard Rail

Your biggest risk isn't laziness. It's building the wrong thing for 6 hours and feeling productive. So the rule is:

**Before opening Claude Code, check the three lines.** If what you're about to do isn't Top 1, you need a reason. Not a feeling. A reason.

## What About Time Blocking Specifically?

I'd skip traditional time blocking for now. Here's why: your schedule is too variable for fixed blocks, and rigid systems create decision fatigue when they break (which they will, every week). The day-type system gives you structure without fragility.

If you want more granularity later, we can add 90-minute sprint blocks on BUILD days. But start with the two-type system and the nightly check-in first. Add complexity only after the simple version is running for two weeks.
