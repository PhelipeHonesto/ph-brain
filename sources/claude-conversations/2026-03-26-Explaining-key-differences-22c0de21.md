## Claude

The user is showing me their Beehiiv billing page for Loud Hawk and wants me to explain the differences between the plans.You're on the **Launch** plan (free) right now. Here's how the four tiers break down:

**Launch ($0)** — Your current plan. You get up to 2,500 subscribers, custom websites/newsletters, campaign analytics, recommendation network, audience segmentation, custom domains, and API access. It's solid for getting started but has Beehiiv branding and no automation.

**Scale ($49/mo)** — Adds everything you'd need to start operating Loud Hawk more professionally: email automations, referral program, Ad Network and Boost Network (monetization), 3D Analytics, beehiiv AI writing tools, segment sends, surveys, community access, and up to 3 teammates. Subscriber tiers start at 1,000 but scale up with pricing.

**Max ($109/mo)** — Adds the premium layer: audio newsletters, AI social helper, direct sponsorships, dynamic content, removes Beehiiv branding entirely, up to 10 publications, unlimited teammates, and priority support. This is where you'd go if you're running multiple newsletter brands under one roof or need white-label polish.

**Enterprise (custom)** — Concierge onboarding, designated account manager, infinite subscribers. Only relevant at serious scale.

For where Loud Hawk is right now (Edition 1 just launched, early traction phase), the free Launch tier makes sense. The moment you'd want to upgrade to Scale is when you need **email automations** (welcome sequences, drip campaigns for new subscribers) or want to tap into the **Ad Network/Boost Network** for cross-promotion and monetization. With your content system already running across Beehiiv, LinkedIn, and Instagram, automations would be the first real unlock worth paying for.

The user wants to know what the Max plan specifically adds beyond Scale.Max gets you everything on Scale plus:

- **Audio newsletters** — auto-generate audio versions of your editions, useful if you want Loud Hawk consumed as a listen-while-you-fly format
- **AI social helper** — helps repurpose newsletter content for social posts (though you already have your Claude Skills pipeline for this)
- **Direct sponsorships** — built-in tools to manage sponsor placements directly, relevant once Loud Hawk has enough readership to sell ad slots
- **Dynamic content** — show different content blocks to different subscriber segments within the same send (e.g., broker-facing vs. pilot-facing content in one edition)
- **Remove Beehiiv branding** — clean white-label experience on loudhawk.beehiiv.com
- **NewsletterXP Course** — their educational content on growing newsletters
- **Unlimited teammates** — Scale caps at 3
- **Up to 10 Publications** — you could run separate newsletters for each OSA brand if needed
- **Priority Support**

The two features that would actually matter for you right now are **dynamic content** (segmenting what brokers vs. operators vs. pilots see) and **removing Beehiiv branding** for a more professional look. The sponsorship tools become relevant once you're monetizing. Everything else is either redundant with your existing stack or premature at this subscriber count.

At $109/mo it's a big jump from free. I'd say Scale at $49 is the natural next step first, and Max only when you're actively selling sponsorships or need segmented content blocks.

## Claude

The user is asking about dynamic content in Beehiiv's Max plan.Dynamic content lets you send one newsletter but show different content blocks to different subscribers based on tags, segments, or attributes you've set up.

So imagine you send Loud Hawk Edition 5. Inside that single send:

- A subscriber tagged as **"broker"** sees a section on deal flow trends and a CTA to reach out to OSA Connect
- A subscriber tagged as **"pilot"** sees a section on fleet intel and type-rating market data
- A subscriber tagged as **"operator"** sees pricing benchmarks and demand signals

Same edition, same send, but each reader gets the version most relevant to them. You're not maintaining three separate newsletters — you're just toggling blocks on/off per segment.

The practical value for Loud Hawk is that your audience isn't monolithic. A charter broker cares about different things than an aviation enthusiast or a potential aircraft buyer. Dynamic content lets you speak to each without diluting the experience for the others, and without tripling your production workload.

That said, you'd need enough subscribers in each segment for it to matter. Right now with early traction, a single unified edition is the right move. Dynamic content becomes worth it once you have identifiable clusters in your list and enough volume that personalization moves the needle on engagement.
