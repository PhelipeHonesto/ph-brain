## Claude

Great questions. Let me break down how Pentagram actually operates, because their model is genuinely unusual.

**How working with them works**

Pentagram is structured as a flat partnership of equals — currently around 24 partner-designers who each own an equal share of the firm and split income equally. When you hire Pentagram, you're not hiring "the firm" in an abstract sense. You're hiring a specific partner. The partners themselves are the primary contact for every client and the ones actually creating the work — not handing it off to a junior team while they go play golf. Each partner runs their own team of designers underneath them, but the creative direction comes from the top.

So the engagement typically looks like: you'd approach Pentagram (or more likely, a specific partner whose work resonates with your brand), go through a discovery and scoping process, and if there's a fit, that partner personally leads your project from strategy through execution. Paula Scher has noted that some of her best work comes fast — bold strokes — but clients sometimes feel uneasy because they expect to "buy process" and feel like they're getting their money's worth over time. That tension between speed-of-genius and perceived value is baked into working at this level.

**What makes them so expensive**

A few things stack up. First, you're paying for the partner's personal reputation and decades of accumulated taste — these are people who've shaped how major global brands look and feel. Second, the scope of what they deliver is massive: brand identity, brand strategy, campaigns, digital experiences, packaging, publications, signage, environmental graphics, custom typefaces, motion graphics, and industrial design. Third, at this tier you're paying for work that needs to function across every conceivable touchpoint for potentially millions of daily impressions — the stakes and complexity are just different than a startup rebrand.

And frankly, the pricing reflects the clientele. Citibank had $700 billion in assets under management when they hired Pentagram — at that scale, even a $10 million rebrand is a rounding error relative to the brand equity it protects.

**What you'd actually get**

At this tier, a full engagement could include brand strategy, naming, messaging architecture, a complete visual identity system, website design, and advertising campaign development. Practically, that means you'd walk away with things like: a logo and mark system, a custom or curated type system, a color palette and visual language, brand guidelines documenting how everything works together, templates and collateral, possibly a custom typeface, and potentially environmental or packaging design depending on scope.

The deliverable isn't a logo file — it's an entire operating system for how your brand shows up in the world.

**The OSA lens**

Honestly, what Pentagram delivers at $500K+ is conceptually the same thing you're building across OSA's four brands right now — just at a radically different scale and polish level. The architecture you've set up (parent brand, OSA Connect, Loud Hawk, OSA Aircraft as interconnected but distinct identities) is the same kind of brand system thinking Pentagram does. You're just doing it founder-style with your designer instead of paying seven figures. Which is exactly right for this stage.
