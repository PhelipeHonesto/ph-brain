**Q1: Did you review the CBT section related to accidents and incidents relating to airplane upsets?**
Yes. (Just a compliance acknowledgment.)

**Q2: Pilots can utilize trend vectors to better control the aircraft's attitude. By including the trend vectors in the instrument scan, pilots are able to precisely control airspeed and altitude.**
**True.** The CBT (page 66) states exactly this: trend vectors help pilots precisely control airspeed and altitude.

**Q3: Referencing the Upset Recovery Procedure, which step requires the pilot to manage the lift vector orientation and requires maneuvering in the shortest direction of the sky pointer?**
**ROLL.** Per SOPM Ch 5: "Manage the lift vector orientation by rolling toward the sky pointer the shortest direction to wings level."

**Q4: It is acceptable to perform cyclical or oscillatory control inputs. No concern is needed in regard to exceeding the structural limits of the airplane.**
**False.** The SOPM and CBT both explicitly say to avoid cyclical or oscillatory control inputs to prevent exceeding structural limits.

**Q5: Which recovery procedure is used during a stall?**
**Upset recovery procedure.** Per SOPM: "The upset recovery procedure is the same for all upsets, including an impending or full stall."

**Q6: The Master Warning and Master Caution switchlights are reset by pushing the respective light on either glareshield. This cancels the aural warning, resets, and rearms the system for future messages and alerts.**
**True.** CBT page 93 confirms this exactly.

**Q7: Either pilot may call a go-around. The immediate response is the execution of a missed approach procedure. When the airspeed has dropped below ____, initiate the missed approach procedure and retract flaps only when the airspeed becomes greater than ____.**
**Vref.** Per SOPM: "If the airspeed has dropped below VREF, initiate the go-around procedure, and retract flaps only when the airspeed becomes greater than VREF."

**Q8: A stall is usually accompanied by a continuous stall warning, and it is characterized by ____.**
**All of the above.** CBT page 31 lists stall characteristics as: buffeting (which could be heavy), lack of pitch authority, lack of roll control, and inability to arrest descent rate.

**Q9: Instructors and pilots should be mindful of the technical and physiological boundaries when using a particular Flight Simulation Training Device (FSTD) for upset training.**
**True.** CBT page 7 states this directly.

**Q10: Early recognition of erroneous airspeed indications require familiarity with the relationship of attitude, thrust setting, and airspeed... pilots must be familiar with the approximate ____ and ____ for each phase of flight.**
**pitch attitude, thrust setting.** CBT page 78: "pilots must be familiar with the approximate pitch attitude and thrust setting for each phase of flight."

**Q11: Which of the following would meet the definition of an upset?**
**All of the above.** Per SOPM/CBT: pitch >25 nose up, pitch >10 nose down, and bank >45 all qualify.

**Q12: If a TAWS warning is encountered during recovery from a low altitude stall event, recovery from the ____ warning should take precedence.**
**stall.** CBT page 43: "recovery from the stall warning should take precedence." Then execute the TAWS escape after recovering from the stall.

**Q13: Which system malfunction(s) can contribute to an upset?**
**All of the above.** CBT page 22 covers flight instrument anomalies, flight control anomalies, and autoflight systems anomalies as causes of upsets.

**Q14: Referencing the Upset Recovery Procedure, which step requires the pilot to manage angle-of-attack, ensures recovery from the stall, and increase roll effectiveness?**
**PUSH.** Per SOPM: PUSH reduces AOA, and "unloading the airplane improves roll effectiveness even when beyond 90 degrees of bank."

**Q15: Which document contains the description for the Upset Recovery Procedure?**
**SkyWest SOPM SP 3700.** The upset recovery procedure is in SOPM Chapter 5. CBT page 45 references this.

**Q16: It is essential to accurately recognize what is happening and then apply the correct control inputs. Inappropriate control inputs can lead to a different upset, structural damage, or unrecoverable flight.**
**True.** CBT page 17 states this verbatim.

**Q17: Referencing the Upset Recovery Procedure, which step requires the pilot to return the aircraft to a normal flight path, configure the aircraft as necessary, return to an appropriate level of automation, and implementing the TEAM after the aircraft has returned to a safe state?**
**STABILIZE.** Per SOPM/CBT: "Return to a normal flight path... After stabilizing the aircraft, implement the TEAM as applicable."

**Q18: Referencing the Upset Recovery Procedure, which step requires the pilot to add thrust as necessary, extend spoilers as necessary, and manage the aircraft energy?**
**THRUST.** Per SOPM: "Manage aircraft energy. Use thrust and speedbrakes as required."

**Q19: Most jet transport airplanes are thrust limited, rather than low speed buffet limited, at altitude, especially in a turn. It is imperative that crews be aware of outside temperature and thrust available.**
**True.** CBT page 10 states this directly.

**Q20: What procedure should you first implement if you stall while flying an approach?**
**Approach Stall Recovery Procedure.** Since a stall is treated as an upset, you use the upset recovery procedure. But the key here is that the stall takes priority. The answer is **Upset Recovery Procedure** since the SOPM states the upset recovery procedure is used for all stalls.
