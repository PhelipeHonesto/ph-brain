Found it.Kallaway's Dopamine Ladder is a six-level framework for engineering viewer attention in short-form content. The six stages release increasing amounts of dopamine, and the goal is to move viewers up the ladder to drive full watch-through, repeat views, and a Pavlovian attention response.

The six rungs:

1. **Stimulation** — The first 1–2 seconds. Use motion, color, contrast, and brightness to visually stun viewers and prevent feed desensitization.

2. **Captivation** — The curiosity stage. Open an internal question in the viewer's mind using a surprising premise or contrast to create a curiosity loop.

3. **Anticipation** — The viewer starts guessing possible answers, and dopamine peaks just before the reveal. Use misdirection or sequential questions to extend the loop.

4. **Validation** — Closing the loop releases dopamine and satisfaction.

5. **Affection** — The viewer begins to like and trust the creator. The strongest driver of likability is solving real problems and providing genuine value.

6. **Revelation** — The viewer recognizes the creator as a consistent, reliable source of value, creating a Pavlovian response where just seeing the creator triggers dopamine.

The big takeaway for your HAWK content and Loud Hawk: levels 1–4 happen within a single piece of content, but levels 5–6 compound across multiple pieces. That's why consistency and repeatedly solving a specific niche problem (e.g., "how do I charter a jet without getting ripped off") accelerates the bonding loop way faster than entertainment alone.
