# FOUNDER STORY = Reframing unconventional career path on LinkedIn

*Summary: **Conversation Overview**

Phelipe is the founder and CEO of Orange Sunshine Aviation (OSA), a multi-brand aviation platform he is building while working as a First Officer on the Embraer E175 at SkyWest Airlines. OSA encompasses several verticals including Loud Hawk (an aviation intelligence newsletter), OSA Connect (charter brokerage), OSA Aircraft, OSA Management, and OSA Capital. The domain is orangesunshine.com. Phelipe holds an ATP certificate with CFI/CFII/MEI ratings and approximately 2,500+ hours across turbine, multi-engine, and single-engine aircraft. He studied Control and Automation Engineering at UFLA (Universidade Federal de Lavras) in Brazil before leaving the program, then spent roughly three years in Denver working in hospitality and restaurant operations before entering aviation. He is based in Illinois and is targeting a product launch around April 21 with a YC Summer 2026 application deadline of May 4.

The conversation focused on two main areas: optimizing Phelipe's LinkedIn profile and conducting a strategic fundraising and team-building analysis. On LinkedIn, Claude reviewed his existing profile (which had 67 connections and lacked an About section) and recommended changes including: updating his headline to lead with OSA founder positioning, rewriting the OSA description away from "staged aviation advisory company" to "AI-powered charter brokerage," adding an About section that leads with his personal arc (Brazil → Denver → cockpit → founder), adding a Denver operations and hospitality experience entry below his CFI role chronologically, adding UFLA under Education as "Control and Automation Engineering (coursework)," and growing connections aggressively toward 500+. Phelipe confirmed he has since added an About section, though Claude assessed it as still missing his origin story and using hedging language like "being developed in stages" that should be removed. His domain is orangesunshine.com, which Claude noted carries stronger client trust than alternatives.

On the strategic side, the conversation covered Phelipe's founder investability, the co-founder versus founding engineer distinction, YC reapplication strategy, and hiring sequencing. Claude assessed Phelipe as a credible but not yet investable solo founder — strong on domain expertise, grit narrative, and sales ability, but presenting a risk profile due to being a non-technical solo founder with no revenue yet and limited audience. The consensus reached was that Phelipe should apply to YC on May 4 as a free "plant a flag" move, close deals to generate revenue before the interview stage, and if not accepted, use the May-through-August window to generate revenue sufficient to fund a founding engineer hire (estimated at financial considerations in the range of a meaningful pre-seed or YC funding), then reapply for Winter 2027 with traction, a founding engineer, and Loud Hawk audience growth. Phelipe understood that YC's co-founder preference is economically motivated — two founders working for equity extends runway — and his counter-argument must be revenue. Claude noted that YC tracks all applications and views reapplication positively when the slope of progress is clear between submissions. Phelipe's preference is to remain sole founder with a founding engineer rather than a 50/50 co-founder, which Claude affirmed as the right call for cap table cleanliness if revenue can support the hire. The UFLA background was discussed as a legitimate signal of cognitive ability and early systems thinking exposure, but Phelipe was clear he is not deeply technical and should not overclaim engineering expertise — his positioning should be "technical enough" domain expert, not technical founder.*
