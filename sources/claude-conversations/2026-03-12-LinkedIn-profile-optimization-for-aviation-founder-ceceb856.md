## Claude

You're right, I've seen that advice floating around and it's worth addressing directly.

The theory is that LinkedIn's algorithm suppresses posts with external links because it wants to keep people on the platform. So people say "link in comments" to try to game the reach.

Here's the reality: it used to matter more than it does now. LinkedIn has changed how it handles links multiple times. The current consensus from people who actually test this is that a link in the post body doesn't kill reach the way it used to, especially if the post itself is good content that gets engagement. A bad post with the link in comments still dies. A good post with the link in the body still performs.

For your situation specifically, I'd put the link in the post body. Here's why:

Your audience is busy professionals, not LinkedIn power users who know to scroll to comments looking for a link. If someone reads your post on mobile, sees it's interesting, and doesn't immediately see where to subscribe, they're gone. You lose the conversion for zero meaningful reach benefit.

The "link in comments" move also looks like you're trying to hack the algorithm, which conflicts with the direct, no-games tone OSA is built on.

If you want to split the difference, you could end the post with "I'll drop the subscribe link below" and then immediately comment with it. That way it's in both places. But honestly, just put it in the post. The content quality matters more than link placement tricks.
