## What This Project Needs Now

The Loud Hawk layer is execution-ready. The OSA Connect operational layer (how to run a trip) is solid. What's completely missing is the **demand generation layer** — the system that puts OSA Connect in front of people who need charter and turns that attention into inquiries.

Given where you are (solo founder, part-time, Year 1, budget ceiling of roughly $3K–$5K total), here's what I'd prioritize building in this project, in order:

**1. OSA Connect Inquiry Follow-Up Email Sequence**
This comes first because it's the highest-leverage thing you don't have. You already have an inquiry intake form and a qualification flow. But there's no written sequence for what happens after someone reaches out and doesn't book immediately. Most charter inquiries don't convert on first contact. A 3–4 email nurture sequence (spaced over 2–3 weeks) that's on-brand and not pushy would capture revenue you're otherwise leaving on the table. This costs nothing and works whether the lead comes from Google, LinkedIn, a referral, or Loud Hawk.

**2. Google Ads Campaign Structure for OSA Connect**
Charter intent search is the most direct path to qualified inquiries. Someone searching "private jet charter [city pair]" or "charter flight broker" has a real trip in mind. A tight Google Ads account structure — campaigns, ad groups, keywords, and ad copy that respect the 30/90 character limits — would give you a paid demand channel you can turn on at low spend ($500–$1,000/month to start) and measure quickly. This needs keyword research, negative keyword lists, ad copy variations, and a clear landing page destination.

**3. OSA Connect Landing Page Copy**
Google Ads (or any paid channel) needs somewhere to send people. Right now the Execution Memo says the Year 1 website just needs to "prove existence." That's fine for a business card site, but if you're spending money on ads, the landing page needs to convert. This means a charter-focused page with a clear value proposition, trust signals, and a single call to action (request a quote / tell us about your trip). Not a full website redesign — one conversion-focused page.

**4. LinkedIn Organic Playbook for OSA Connect (Separate from Loud Hawk)**
Loud Hawk has its own LinkedIn strategy. But OSA Connect as a business also benefits from founder-led LinkedIn presence that's more directly commercial — sharing how you think about charter, responding to industry conversations, and being visible to the B2B audience (business owners, family offices, wealth advisors) who might refer or use you. This is different from the Loud Hawk editorial voice. It's the advisor voice.

**5. SEO Content Brief Library**
Longer-term, but worth mapping now: 10–15 high-intent pages targeting queries like "how much does a private charter cost," "charter broker vs booking direct," "what to look for in a charter operator." These pages build organic traffic over 6–12 months and compound. They also double as credibility assets you can link to from emails and social.
