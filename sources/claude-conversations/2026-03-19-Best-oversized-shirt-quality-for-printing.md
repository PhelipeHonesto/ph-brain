Good breakdown from the search. Here's what I'd recommend for OSA specifically, thinking about the brand positioning (premium, aviation-adjacent, dark aesthetic for Loud Hawk):

---

**Top picks for OSA branded shirts:**

**1. Comfort Colors 1717 (Garment-Dyed Heavyweight)**
The gold standard for premium branded merch right now. Garment-dyed so every shirt has a slightly unique, lived-in look. Boxy, relaxed fit -- not technically "oversized" but reads that way. 100% ring-spun cotton with a soft hand feel, prints beautifully. Used by high-end brands and restaurants. This would be my #1 pick for OSA -- it signals quality without trying too hard.

**2. Los Angeles Apparel 1801GD**
LA Apparel is a specialty brand known for boutique-quality blanks and this is their flagship heavy tee. 6.5 oz, genuinely oversized boxy cut, 100% cotton. It's what a lot of serious streetwear brands actually use. Pricier per unit (~$15-20 blank) but the quality difference is immediately noticeable.

**3. Stanley/Stella Rocker (STTU758)**
Made from 100% organic cotton, around 200 GSM, with a true oversized streetwear cut. European brand with strong sustainability credentials -- relevant if OSA ever wants to lean into that angle. Very popular with premium merch right now.

**4. Bella + Canvas 3001**
The industry workhorse. Known for soft, fashion-forward, high-quality blanks and extremely consistent sizing. Not as boxy as LA Apparel but works well if you want a more fitted-oversized look (size up). Best value-to-quality ratio.

---

**For OSA specifically I'd lean Comfort Colors or LA Apparel.** The garment-dye texture on Comfort Colors pairs well with a dark, worn-in aesthetic that would fit Loud Hawk branding. LA Apparel is the move if you want something that genuinely looks like a streetwear drop.

What's the use case -- crew gifts, merch to sell, event giveaways? That'd help me narrow it down further on quantity and print method.

## Claude

Then **Los Angeles Apparel 1801GD** is your answer. It's what serious brands actually use when quality is the only metric that matters.

Here's why it wins:

**The shirt itself** -- 6.5 oz heavyweight 100% cotton, genuine boxy oversized cut with dropped shoulders, thick collar that holds its shape after washing. When someone picks it up they immediately feel the difference versus a Bella+Canvas or Gildan. It's the kind of shirt people ask "where'd you get this."

**Print quality** -- The heavy cotton takes screen printing and DTG exceptionally well. Colors sit on top of the fabric instead of soaking in, so designs stay vibrant longer.

**The brand signal** -- LA Apparel is made in Los Angeles, which carries weight. When you hand someone an OSA shirt and they look it up, the blank itself has cachet. That matters for a premium aviation brand.

**The only tradeoff** -- Cost. Expect to pay $15-22 per blank before printing. But if you're putting the OSA or Loud Hawk mark on something and handing it to a potential client or operator contact, the shirt being cheap undercuts everything else you've built.

For printing, pair it with **water-based or discharge ink** (not plastisol) -- it keeps the soft hand feel and gives a more premium, almost vintage result. Avoid the stiff plastisol print that screams promotional giveaway.

If you ever want a second tier option for higher volume, Comfort Colors 1717 is the step down -- still excellent, just a different aesthetic.
