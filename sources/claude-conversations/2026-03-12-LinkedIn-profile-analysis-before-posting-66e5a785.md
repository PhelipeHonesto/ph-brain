## Claude

This is a strong first post, Phelipe. It's opinionated, specific, and shows insider knowledge without being preachy. A few thoughts to make it hit harder:

**What's working:**
The opening line is a great hook — it calls out a real industry problem immediately. The middle section explaining why aircraft selection matters gives you credibility without bragging. And the soft CTA at the end doesn't feel pushy.

**Tweaks I'd suggest:**

**Opening — tighten the hook.** The first line is good but "quote you a price" could be sharper. Consider:
> *Most charter brokers give you a number before they even ask where you're going.*

Feels more conversational and punchy for a LinkedIn scroll.

**Middle section — break it up more.** LinkedIn feeds reward short paragraphs and white space. That second paragraph is doing a lot of work. I'd split it:

> The aircraft that fits a 4-seat, 800nm domestic trip is fundamentally different from the one that fits a 2-seat, 300nm same-day turn.
>
> The operator matters. The route matters. The mission matters.
>
> But most of the industry skips straight to the number because that's what closes fast.

That "operator matters" line hits way harder standing on its own.

**The Loud Hawk intro — add one concrete example.** Instead of listing topics generally, tease one recent or upcoming piece. Something like: "In the latest issue, I broke down why [specific topic]." Gives people a reason to click *now*.

**CTA — make it slightly warmer.** Instead of "it might be worth a read," try something like "Link's below — I'd love to hear what you think." Invites engagement, which helps the algorithm push the post.

**One last thing:** post this on a Tuesday or Wednesday morning, around 7-9 AM Chicago time. That's when aviation professionals are scrolling before their day gets busy. And spend 20-30 minutes engaging on other posts right before and after you publish — the algorithm rewards that activity window.
