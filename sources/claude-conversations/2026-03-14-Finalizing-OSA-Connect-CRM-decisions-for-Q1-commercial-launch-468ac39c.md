## DECISION 1: MARGIN POLICY

**Recommendation:** 10% standard floor on gross charter cost. Allow 7–12% flex range depending on trip complexity and operator commission structure.

**Industry standard:** Charter broker margins typically range from 5–15% of gross charter cost. Larger brokerages with overhead and marketing budgets often push toward 15–20%. Boutique and solo operators commonly land at 7–12%. Your Execution Memo v5 models financial scenarios at 7% of gross bookings, which is conservative and realistic for planning.

**What makes sense for Year 1 solo operator:** Start at 10% as your default. On a $25,000 light jet booking, that's $2,500 gross to OSA Connect. On competitive quotes where you're being shopped, you can flex down to 7%. On complex trips with high coordination (multi-leg, catering, ground transport), flex up to 12%. Never go below 7% — that's your floor. Below that you're working for free after your time costs.

**How it works operationally:** Operators quote you a wholesale price. You add your margin to produce the client price. Some operators build broker commission into their quote (typically 5–10%), meaning you receive a commission rather than marking up. Document which model each operator uses in your Operator Database. Your Quote Comparison Template (Tool 4 in your Operating System) already has fields for "Your commission ($)" and "Client net price ($)" — use them consistently.

**Flag for aviation lawyer:** Have the charter agreement clearly state that OSA Connect earns a brokerage fee/commission, and that the client price includes this fee. Transparency here aligns with your locked Decision 6 (full operator disclosure). The agreement should not promise "at cost" or "no markup" pricing. Also confirm your fee language complies with DOT Part 295 disclosure requirements for air charter brokers.

**LOCKED: 10% standard margin floor. Flex range 7–12%. Never below 7%.**

## DECISION 2: PAYMENT SPLIT POLICY

**Recommendation:** 50% deposit upon booking confirmation. Balance due 7 calendar days before departure. 100% due at booking for trips booked within 7 days of departure.

**Industry standard:** Most operators require 50% deposit to confirm, balance 7–14 days pre-departure. Some require 100% upfront for short-lead bookings (under 72 hours). Your Operating System Tool 3 already notes "standard: 25–50% deposit to confirm, balance 7–14 days before departure."

**What makes sense for Year 1 solo operator:** You have zero working capital. You cannot float operator costs. Your payment terms to the client must align with or precede operator payment deadlines. The safest structure is 50/50 with the balance due early enough that you've collected full payment before the operator's final payment deadline. Seven days pre-departure gives you a buffer. For short-notice trips (within 7 days), require 100% upfront — this is standard and no sophisticated client will object.

**Operationally:** Accept wire transfers only in Year 1. Credit card processing introduces fees (2.5–3.5%) that eat your margin and adds chargeback risk. Wire transfers also signal seriousness — a client willing to wire $15,000 is a real buyer. Add ACH as an option in Year 2 if client volume justifies it.

**Flag for aviation lawyer:** The charter agreement must specify that deposits are non-refundable under the cancellation terms (see Decision 3), that OSA Connect acts as payment intermediary, and that funds are forwarded to the operator upon booking confirmation. Confirm the agreement addresses what happens if the operator cancels (client refund minus any non-recoverable costs). Also ensure compliance with DOT requirements regarding client refund rights at the time of operator disclosure.

**LOCKED: 50% deposit at booking. Balance due 7 days pre-departure. 100% upfront for trips booked within 7 days. Wire transfer only in Year 1.**

## DECISION 3: CANCELLATION TERMS

**Recommendation:** Tiered structure aligned with operator cancellation policies.

The structure: cancellation more than 14 days before departure forfeits 25% of total charter price (covers OSA Connect's time and operator cancellation fees). Cancellation 7–14 days before departure forfeits 50%. Cancellation within 7 days of departure forfeits 100%. No-show or day-of cancellation forfeits 100% with no exceptions.

**Industry standard:** Most operators and brokerages use a tiered model. PrivateFly's published terms show 50% after contract execution and 100% within 2 weeks of departure. One-way flights and peak-period bookings at many brokerages are non-refundable after contracting. The common pattern is increasingly severe penalties as departure approaches, because operators commit crew, fuel, and positioning resources.

**What makes sense for Year 1 solo operator:** You cannot absorb a cancellation loss. If a client cancels and the operator keeps their payment, you're out your time and potentially out of pocket. Your cancellation terms should mirror or be slightly more conservative than the operator's terms for that specific trip. The tiered structure above is competitive with industry norms while protecting you.

**Critical nuance:** Your terms to the client should always be at least as strict as the operator's terms to you. Before quoting, confirm the operator's cancellation policy and build your client terms to match or exceed it. If an operator requires 100% non-refundable for a peak-period one-way, your client terms must reflect that.

**Flag for aviation lawyer:** This is the most legally sensitive decision. The charter agreement must clearly state the cancellation schedule in the specific booking confirmation (not just in general terms), that cancellation fees are "agreed compensation" not penalties, and what happens in force majeure scenarios (weather, operator mechanical issues). The lawyer should also address the client's refund rights under DOT Part 295 upon operator disclosure, and confirm that you're not creating a cancellation policy that conflicts with the operator's terms flowing through to the client.

**LOCKED: 25% forfeited if 14+ days out. 50% forfeited if 7–14 days. 100% forfeited if under 7 days. Always match or exceed operator terms for specific trip. Lawyer to finalize exact language.**

## DECISION 4: FORM-TO-CRM AUTOMATION

**Recommendation:** Google Form → Zapier → HubSpot. One-step automation.

**What the options are:** Zapier (most mature integration library, free tier handles low volume), Make/Integromat (slightly cheaper at scale, more complex UI), or HubSpot native forms (simplest but less flexible on the intake side).

**What makes sense for Year 1 solo operator:** Zapier's free tier allows 100 tasks/month. At your volume (0–5 inquiries per month early on), this is more than sufficient for the next 12+ months. The automation is simple: Google Form submission → Zapier creates a Contact in HubSpot with the form fields mapped, and simultaneously creates a Deal at stage "INQUIRY." You already have the Inquiry Intake Form (Tool 1) designed — replicate those fields in a Google Form embedded on your website.

**Setup (one afternoon):** Create a Google Form matching your Inquiry Intake fields (name, email, phone, departure city, destination, dates, PAX count, aircraft preference, special requirements, how they found OSA). Connect Zapier trigger "New Google Form Response" to HubSpot action "Create Contact + Create Deal." Map fields. Test with a dummy submission. Done.

**Why not HubSpot native forms:** HubSpot forms work fine but lock you into HubSpot's form builder. Google Forms gives you a standalone URL you can embed anywhere — website, LinkedIn bio, email signature — without HubSpot dependencies. Keeps optionality if you switch CRMs later.

**LOCKED: Google Form → Zapier free tier → HubSpot (Create Contact + Create Deal at INQUIRY stage).**

## DECISION 5: PRIMARY CRM SYSTEM

**Recommendation:** HubSpot Free CRM as the deal pipeline and client database. Your existing Notion/Operating System as the ops reference layer.

**Reasoning:** HubSpot Free gives you contact management, deal pipeline, email tracking, and basic reporting at zero cost. It scales well. Your Operating System was built in a document/Notion format — that stays as your SOP library and operational playbook. They serve different functions: HubSpot tracks who and when (contacts, deals, pipeline stages). Your OS docs tell you how (SOPs, checklists, templates).

**What makes sense for Year 1 solo operator:** Don't try to make one tool do everything. HubSpot handles CRM. Your Operating System handles execution checklists. Copy/paste between them is fine at your volume. In Year 2, if volume justifies it, you can upgrade to HubSpot Starter ($15/month) for sequences and slightly better automation.

**Pipeline stages (already defined in your Implementation Assets):** INQUIRY → QUOTING → OPTIONS SENT → CONFIRMED → BOOKED → COMPLETED → LOST. These map cleanly to HubSpot's deal pipeline.

**LOCKED: HubSpot Free CRM for deal pipeline + contact database. Existing OS documents for execution SOPs. No paid CRM until volume justifies it.**

## DECISION 7: TRIP PACKET FORMAT

**Recommendation:** Branded PDF attached to email. Not a formatted email body. Not a Notion link.

**Reasoning:** A PDF is downloadable, forwardable, printable, and works offline in a car on the way to the FBO. A formatted email can get clipped or reformatted by email clients. A Notion link requires internet access and looks like an internal tool, not a professional deliverable. The Trip Packet is your highest-touch client communication — it should feel polished and self-contained.

**How to produce it at zero cost:** Create a Google Docs template matching your Pre-Departure Brief (Tool 6). For each trip, duplicate the template, fill in the fields, export as PDF, attach to your email. Time per trip: 10–15 minutes. In Year 2, you can automate this with a Docs template linked to HubSpot deal fields, or build it in Canva with your brand assets.

**What the PDF should include (from your existing Tool 6):** Trip header with OSA Connect branding, departure details (date, time, aircraft, tail number, operator), FBO name and address with arrival guidance, crew names, catering summary, what to bring (ID requirements), your direct contact number, operator dispatch number. Add the Flight Safety Brief (per your locked Decision 6 on transparency). One page front and back, or two clean pages.

**LOCKED: Branded PDF generated from Google Docs template, attached to email 48–72 hours pre-departure.**

## DECISION 8: CATERING SOURCING

**Recommendation:** Operator-managed catering by default. OSA Connect specifies requirements and confirms delivery, but does not arrange catering independently.

**Industry standard:** In most charter bookings, catering is arranged through the operator or the departure FBO. Operators have existing relationships with catering vendors at their base airports. Brokers typically relay client preferences (dietary restrictions, beverage requests, meal level) and the operator executes. Some high-end brokerages arrange independent catering for ultra-premium trips, but this adds complexity, liability, and cost.

**What makes sense for Year 1 solo operator:** You don't have catering vendor relationships, you can't inspect food quality, and you don't want the liability of sourcing food for passengers. Let the operator handle it. Your role is to collect client preferences during intake ("Any dietary restrictions? Standard refreshments or full meal service?"), communicate those to the operator clearly, and confirm catering is on board during your pre-departure confirmation call.

**How it works in your SOP:** During Step 1.5 of your Trip Workflow (contacting operators), include catering requirements in the trip spec. During Step 3.3 (pre-departure brief), confirm with the operator that catering has been ordered and matches the client's request. Include a catering summary line in the Trip Packet.

**When to reconsider:** If you're booking heavy jets or ultra-premium trips where clients expect specific catering (a particular restaurant, wine selection, etc.), you may need to coordinate directly with FBO catering departments. That's a Year 2+ capability — build those vendor relationships as they become relevant.

**Flag for aviation lawyer:** Confirm the charter agreement states that catering is provided by the operator/FBO and that OSA Connect is not liable for food quality, allergens, or catering failures. This is standard broker language but needs to be explicit.

**LOCKED: Operator handles catering. OSA Connect specifies requirements, confirms delivery, and documents in Trip Packet. No independent catering sourcing in Year 1.**

### CLAIM 1: "Response within 4 hours"

**Can you deliver this today?** Partially — and with a critical asterisk.

Your Operating System commits to acknowledging the client within 2 hours (Step 1.2) and delivering quotes within 24 hours. But you are a Part 121 First Officer. On your off-schedule days, a 4-hour response is easy. On flying days, it's physically impossible unless you mean "acknowledgment" (an auto-reply or quick text at the gate) rather than "substantive quote response."

**What you actually need from operators to deliver this:** Nothing extra. This is about your personal availability, not operator agreements.

**Honest website version:** Don't promise a clock. Promise the behavior.

> *"Every inquiry gets a personal response — not a bot, not a queue. We acknowledge within hours and deliver options within one business day."*

This is true on your off days and defensible on your flying days with a quick acknowledgment text during a layover. If you later add a virtual assistant or second broker, you can tighten the SLA.

### CLAIM 2: "All-in transparent pricing (number at quote = number on invoice)"

**Can you deliver this today?** Yes — with one operational caveat.

Your Quote Comparison Template (Tool 4) already requires itemized quotes from operators: base charter rate, fuel surcharge, landing/ramp fees, catering, FBO fees, repositioning. The caveat: de-icing charges and certain international handling fees are genuinely unpredictable at quote time. Even PrivateFly discloses that de-icing is not included in on-demand quotes. You need to either absorb small variances or disclose the possibility upfront.

**What you need from operators:** Itemized quotes (already required in your vetting checklist, Tool 3 Section C). Most operators provide this by default when a broker asks clearly.
