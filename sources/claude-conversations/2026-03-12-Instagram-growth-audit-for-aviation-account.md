Here's the full audit. Let me hit the key findings directly:

**The biggest problem is the content mix, not the posting frequency.** The daily aviation news headline cards are working against you — they attract planespotters and avgeeks, not charter clients. And they violate your own Editorial Blueprint rule about never reposting news without adding value. That content type needs to stop immediately.

**The carousel format is your winner.** The "35h Shortcut That Ends Up Costing $200K" style post — opinionated, multi-slide, Loud Hawk voice — is exactly what works on Instagram in 2026 and aligns with your strongest editorial lane (The Gap). **The newsletter is your content engine.** Each Loud Hawk edition should generate 2-3 Instagram posts by repurposing the sharpest insights. Stop creating Instagram content from scratch daily — repurpose what you've already written.

## Claude

Good news — you already implemented the bio language from the audit. Looking at the screenshot:

**Bio as it stands:**
`Private aviation intelligence Charter market signals · Operator reality · What the industry won't say Newsletter every 2 weeks ↓`

**Format fix — yes, tighten it.** Instagram bios render as a single block, so line breaks matter. Right now "Private aviation intelligence" runs into "Charter market signals" without a clear visual break. Try:

```
Private aviation intelligence.
Charter market signals · Operator reality · What the industry won't say.
Newsletter every 2 weeks ↓
```

The periods give each line a clean stop. Reads sharper.

**On @orangesunshineaviation — no, don't add it.** Three reasons:

First, your Beehiiv Launch Playbook is explicit on this: the sender name is "Loud Hawk," not your personal name, not OSA. The editorial brand stands on its own. The same logic applies to Instagram — Loud Hawk's job is to build credibility and trust before anyone ever needs a broker. Tagging OSA in the bio turns an intelligence brand into a company page.

Second, the Editorial Blueprint's CTA philosophy says attribution, not promotion. The connection to OSA lives in the newsletter footer and on the subscribe page. If someone follows the bio link to Beehiiv, they'll see "Loud Hawk is published by Orange Sunshine Aviation." That's the right place for it — earned discovery, not a billboard.

Third, practically — you have four lines of bio space and every character matters for keyword searchability. "Charter market signals," "operator reality," and "what the industry won't say" all work harder for discovery than an @ tag to a separate account.

**One other thing from the screenshot:** your category shows "Media/news company." That's fine — it signals editorial content rather than a service business. Keep it.

**Also worth noting:** 696 profile views in the last 30 days at 50 followers is actually a solid ratio — roughly 14x your follower count. People are finding the profile. The question is whether they're converting to follows or newsletter subscribers once they get there. That's what the bio tightening and Highlights setup will help with.
