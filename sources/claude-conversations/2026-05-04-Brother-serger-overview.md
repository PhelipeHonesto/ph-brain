For most people, the Brother 1034D is the best overall pick — reliable, beginner-friendly, and the most popular Brother serger. It's a 3/4-thread overlock with 22 stitch functions, color-coded threading, differential feed, free-arm conversion, and 1,300 stitches per minute, usually around $200–250.

If you want something heavier-duty for thick fabrics like denim or upholstery, the Brother ST4031HD is the step up. And the 1034DX is a slightly upgraded version of the 1034D worth grabbing if you can find it.

Yes — within Brother's lineup, the ST4031HD is the heavy-duty pick. It handles 12 layers of denim without hesitation, has a carbon steel blade that stays sharp through hundreds of yards of heavy canvas, and a reinforced metal frame that stays planted at full speed. The cutting blade is so strong it meets the G3 rating for industrial-grade standards, and Brother backs it with a 25-year limited warranty.

A few things to know before buying:

- It only does 3 and 4-thread serges — it cannot do a 2-thread serge
- Standard-strength threads tend to break at full speed, so heavier serger thread is recommended
- No free arm, unlike the 1034D — the bed extension is removable but there's no true free-arm function

If you want something more capable, the step up is the Juki MO-1000 or a Baby Lock with air threading, but those run $700–1,500+. For the $300–400 range and heavy-duty work, the ST4031HD is hard to beat.
