## Claude

Yes, they're meaningfully different. Here's the quick comparison:

**Part 121 (scheduled airlines, like SkyWest)**
Governed by the FAR 117 rules you live under: flight duty period (FDP) limits based on report time and number of segments (typically 9–14 hours), max 8–9 hours of actual flight time depending on acclimation, and mandatory 10-hour rest with 8 hours of sleep opportunity. Cumulative limits: 60 hrs flight time/7 days, 100/28 days, 1,000/365 days.

**Part 135 (on-demand charter)**
Still runs under the older §135.267 framework (for unscheduled ops): 10 hours flight time in 24 consecutive hours for two-pilot ops, 14-hour duty day, 10 hours rest. Weekly/monthly/annual caps are 500/800 hrs quarterly and similar structures — looser in some ways, stricter in others. No FDP table, no acclimation rules, rest requirements are simpler.

**The practical differences that matter for OSA**
- 135 pilots can legally fly longer single days but have less sophisticated fatigue protection
- 121 has hard stop times tied to report time and segment count; 135 is more duration-based
- 135 reserve/standby rules are far less defined than 121's short-call/long-call structure
- The FAA has been circling 135 duty reform for years (the Part 135 Duty/Rest ARC) but nothing final yet

## Claude

Focusing just on flight time limits:

**Part 121 (FAR 117)**
- 8 hours in any 24 consecutive hours (1 pilot)
- 9 hours if FDP starts 0500–1959, 8 hours otherwise (2 pilots, unaugmented)
- 100 hours in 672 consecutive hours (28 days)
- 1,000 hours in 365 consecutive calendar days

**Part 135 (§135.267, unscheduled two-pilot ops)**
- 10 hours in any 24 consecutive hours
- 500 hours in any calendar quarter
- 800 hours in any 2 consecutive calendar quarters
- 1,400 hours in any calendar year

**The headline difference**
135 lets you fly 10 hours of stick time in a day vs. 121's 8–9. That's the number most people are asking about when they compare the two. But 135's 14-hour duty day cap is what actually constrains it in practice — you rarely hit 10 flight hours before the duty clock kills you.

135 single-pilot ops are tighter: 8 hours flight time in 24 hours.

## Claude

You're right to call it out. The post is technically accurate on the 135 numbers but misleading in how it frames the 121 comparison.

**What's actually true**
- 135 two-pilot: 14-hour duty, 10 flight hours ✓
- "Started at 7am, flying you at 9pm" — yes, legally possible ✓

**What's misleading**
The "Airlines under Part 121 have stricter rules" line implies a big gap. In reality, 121 FDP under FAR 117 goes up to **13 hours** for an unaugmented two-pilot crew starting mid-morning, and **14 hours** for report times starting around 0500–0659. So a 121 pilot can also be on duty 13–14 hours and fly you late in their day.

The actual 121 vs 135 delta on a single day is roughly:
- **Flight time:** 121 caps at 8–9 hrs, 135 at 10 hrs (1–2 hr difference)
- **Duty time:** basically the same at the upper end (13–14 hrs)
- **Rest:** both require 10 hours

**Where 121 is genuinely stricter**
- FDP scales down based on report time and number of legs (135 doesn't)
- Hard acclimation rules for time zone changes
- Cumulative 100/28 and 1,000/365 caps
- Fitness-for-duty fatigue call protections
- Prescriptive reserve/short-call rules

**Where the post's narrative breaks down**
A 121 regional pilot on a 4-leg day with an early report can absolutely be on hour 13 of duty when they fly the last leg. The "your charter pilot started at 7am" framing applies to airline flying too — anyone who's been on a delayed United flight at 10pm has flown with a crew who started before sunrise.

The honest version of that post: *135 allows 1–2 more flight hours per day and has weaker fatigue science built in (no FDP table, no acclimation, looser reserve rules).* That's a real and defensible point. The way he wrote it makes it sound like 121 pilots clock out at noon.

Classic LinkedIn charter-safety FUD — technically sourced, rhetorically stretched.
