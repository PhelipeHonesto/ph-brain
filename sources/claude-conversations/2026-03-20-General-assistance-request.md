I can see you've got the Leadership V Q126 quiz open. I've got the full presentation material right here. Let me give you the answers:

**Q1.** Long landings, or landing outside of the touchdown zone, is a safety focus area and a concern that the FAA continues to actively monitor. These events often occur on shorter runways.
**True**

**Q2.** On short runways served by a precision approach, some TDZ markings may be omitted.
**True** (Per the presentation: runways shorter than 6,000 feet will have some TDZ markings omitted.)

**Q3.** Which of the following are effective in preventing long landings?
**All of the above**

**Q4.** When runway conditions are less than optimal, pilots should _____ decelerate the aircraft and _____ rolling out to the runway end.
**assertively, avoid**

**Q5.** Airline transport category aircraft may experience reduced or inhibited braking and reverse thrust when landings are excessively soft.
**True**

**Q6.** A go-around, balked, or rejected landing is required when...
**All of the above**

**Q7.** Most commonly, a long landing occurs after a stable approach.
**True** (Straight from the presentation, slide 3.)

**Q8.** FOIB 031524-1 defines the touchdown zone as the first 3,000 feet or the first one _____ of a runway, whichever is less.
**third**

**Q9.** Many runways don't have the full TDZ marking set... An effective mitigation to ensure you land in the TDZ is to brief a _____.
**planned touchdown point** (Per FOIB031542-1, a planned touchdown point must be briefed for every landing.)

**Q10.** As long as the airplane touches down in the painted touchdown zone markings we can assume compliance with landing in the touchdown zone or first 1/3rd of the runway.
**False** (Slide 13 specifically calls this out. Example: a 5,800 ft runway has TDZ markings to 2,500 ft, but 1/3 of the runway is only 1,933 ft. Painted markings don't guarantee compliance.)
