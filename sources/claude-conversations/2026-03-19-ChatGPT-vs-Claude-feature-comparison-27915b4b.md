Good question, Phelipe. Here's an honest rundown of the notable features ChatGPT has that I currently don't:

**Native image generation.** ChatGPT has DALL-E integrated directly into the chat, and it went viral when OpenAI made it available across all tiers. I can search for images on the web but I can't create them from scratch.

**Video generation.** OpenAI is planning to integrate Sora's video generation directly into ChatGPT, and Sora already exists as a standalone app. I have nothing equivalent.

**Voice mode as a core feature.** ChatGPT has a polished real-time voice conversation experience across mobile and web. Claude has some voice capabilities but it's not at the same level of integration.

**Study mode and interactive learning.** ChatGPT added a study mode that uses Socratic questioning, creates practice quizzes, and remembers your skill level across chats. They also launched interactive visual modules for exploring formulas and variables in real time across 70+ math and science topics. I don't have a dedicated learning mode like that.

**Group chats.** ChatGPT supports group chats where up to twenty people can collaborate in a shared conversation with AI. I'm strictly one-on-one.

**Shopping mode.** ChatGPT has a dedicated shopping research mode that compares products, finds deals, and uses memory to personalize recommendations. I can search the web for product info, but I don't have a structured shopping workflow.

**Custom GPTs / GPT Store.** Users can build and share specialized chatbots for specific tasks. Claude has Projects and custom instructions, but not a public marketplace of user-created bots.

**Native app integrations (connectors).** ChatGPT connects to Google Drive, SharePoint, email, CRM, and more through what they now call "Apps." I have some integrations (Google Calendar, Gmail, etc.) but ChatGPT's ecosystem is broader.

**Where I hold my own or have an edge:** file creation (docx, pptx, xlsx, PDF), artifacts/interactive React apps, Claude Code for agentic terminal coding, deep research, Projects with persistent context, and generally stronger performance on long-form reasoning, writing quality, and nuanced analysis. Also worth noting: ChatGPT started testing ads for free and Go-tier users in January 2026, while Claude products remain ad-free.

For what you're building with OSA, the gaps that would matter most are probably image generation (for marketing assets) and maybe the broader connector ecosystem. But for the actual strategic thinking, document creation, and coding work you're doing, you're not missing much.

Yeah, Google is crushing it on both fronts right now. Here's the picture:

**Image generation:** Google's Nano Banana lineup (built into Gemini) now includes models that generate up to 4K images with advanced text rendering, character consistency across prompts, and the ability to use Google Search to ground images in real-time data. Over a billion images were created with Nano Banana Pro in just 53 days. The quality is genuinely impressive, and the pricing is way cheaper than OpenAI's DALL-E at scale. Free Gemini users get 100 images per day with the standard model. ChatGPT's image gen went viral too, but OpenAI literally had to throttle it because it was melting their GPUs.

**Video generation:** This is where Google has the clearest lead. Veo 3.1 generates native synchronized audio alongside video, supports up to 4K resolution, and offers reference-image locking for character consistency. That audio piece is huge. Sora 2 is still considered the realism leader for raw visual quality, especially lighting and physics, but its success rate has dropped and cost per usable clip has gone up. Sora caps at 1080p and doesn't generate audio natively, so you need post-production for sound. Meanwhile Veo 3.1 is accessible through a $19.99/month Gemini Advanced subscription, and Sora 2 lives behind ChatGPT Plus ($20/month) or Pro ($200/month).

The TL;DR: Google wins on accessibility, audio integration, 4K output, and price-to-value on video. Sora 2 edges Google on raw cinematic realism and human motion. For your OSA marketing use cases (Loud Hawk visuals, social content, brand assets), Google's ecosystem would probably give you more bang for your buck since you need volume and consistency more than Hollywood-grade realism.

Here's my honest breakdown of where OpenAI wins against both me and Google:

**Feature breadth / "Swiss Army knife" factor.** ChatGPT is designed to be a single workbench where writing, analysis, file work, and structured transformations can all be handled without switching apps. No one else matches the sheer number of things you can do in one interface: image gen, voice mode, shopping, study mode, group chats, Custom GPTs, Sora video (coming), Canvas for editing, and a massive plugin/connector ecosystem. It's the most complete product.

**Voice mode.** ChatGPT's real-time voice is the most polished of the three. Neither Google nor I are at that level for natural back-and-forth spoken conversation.

**Image generation (vs me).** ChatGPT generates images natively. I don't. Google's Nano Banana is arguably better quality, but ChatGPT still beats me on this by default since I have zero image gen capability.

**Brand recognition and ecosystem size.** ChatGPT has over 200 million weekly users. That massive user base means more integrations, more Custom GPTs, more community knowledge, and faster iteration on features.

**Creative content and marketing copy.** ChatGPT won the examples and case study round by featuring relatable customer stories that created natural opportunities for internal linking. For marketing-style content where you need punchy, conversion-oriented copy, ChatGPT tends to be stronger than both me and Gemini.

**Memory across sessions (vs Google).** ChatGPT's memory system is more mature and actively shapes responses. I have memory too, but ChatGPT's has been in the wild longer and is more aggressively personalized.

**Where it does NOT win:**

I beat ChatGPT on coding (Claude achieves 80.9% on SWE-bench Verified compared to GPT-5.2's roughly 70%), long-form writing quality, nuanced reasoning, and document creation. In a blind test with 134 voters, I won 4 out of 8 rounds, Gemini won 3, and ChatGPT won just 1.

Google beats ChatGPT on image generation quality (Nano Banana), video generation (Veo 3.1 with native audio), context window size (1M tokens), Google Workspace integration, and arguably price-to-value.

For your OSA workflow specifically: you're already using me for the heavy lifting (strategy, coding, document creation, content systems) and cross-referencing with ChatGPT, which is actually the optimal setup. If you ever need quick marketing visuals or want to prototype ad concepts without your designer, ChatGPT or Gemini would fill that gap better than I can.
