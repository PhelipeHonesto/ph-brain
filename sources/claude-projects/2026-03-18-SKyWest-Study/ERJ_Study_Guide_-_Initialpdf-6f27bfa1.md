STUDY GUIDE INFORMATION
This guide is designed to guide self-study and to maximize efficiency when it comes to study time. Each section 
emphasizes important information to help set you up for success during each of your lessons. While this guide does 
cover a majority of the training knowledge, it is not all inclusive and it is meant to support instructor briefings and 
initiate conversation and questions related to each topic. 
Each lesson will have an associated “study assignment” slide. This will help guide your studying to ensure that each 
section of this study guide is reviewed. If you follow the content provided in the “study assignment” slide, it will make 
this study guide much more manageable and help break it down into manageable pieces. This will reduce the stress of 
completing this study guide in its entirety and will also allow you to absorb the information over the span of training.
If you have any questions regarding this study guide, please ask an instructor or any member of ERJ training leadership. 
Further training information and contact information can be found at 
https://www.skywestonline.com/OO/flightoperations/training/erj-training.

ERJ TRAINING OVERVIEW
ERJ training has broken down into multiple phases of training: Ground School, Procedures Training, Maneuvers Training, 
and Line Oriented Flight Training. Each phase of training has an associated validation. Each validation is described below 
with recommended preparation.
PREPARING FOR THE SYSTEMS VALIDATION (SV)
The Systems Validation (SV) is an electronic, multiple choice test that will take place on Initial Ground School Day 5. You 
will be able to use a flightdeck poster during the test that contains each synoptic page (do not write on your flightdeck 
poster). Looking at a synoptic page may help you answer many of the system-intensive questions. Studying these items 
ahead of time will increase the likelihood of a successful, satisfactory score. 
The following systems subtasks from the Pilot Advanced Qualification Program Manual (PAQPM) are validated during 
the Systems Validation (SV): 
Warning System Air Management System Flight Control System Navigation Equipment
Emergency Equipment Auxiliary Power Unit (APU) Fuel Systems Flight Instruments
Engines Hydraulic Systems Communications Equipment Fire Protection System
Electrical System Landing Gear and Brakes Automatic Flight Control 
System (AFCS)
Ice and Rain Protection
System
To assist you in preparing for your SV, please review the SOPM, QRC/QRH, and AOM. The test validates each of these 
sections by asking 10 questions per subtask. The test will ask 160 questions that must be completed within the 2-hour 
time limit. The minimum passing score is 80% overall. The following sections present a sample of questions and topics
to assist in preparation. 
The resources available to be successful on the SV:
• SOPM 
• CBT – Systems CBT’s, ERJ Training Resources
• Ground System’s slideshow/training content
Test Taking Software
The testing software will be loaded onto your SkyWest Online account. The test is composed of all multiple-choice 
questions. It does not contain any select all that apply or fill in the blank questions.
The software has two options after you answer. You can choose to Defer a 
question or Submit a question:
- Defers the question and it will appear again at the end 
of the test. This allows you to review the question and 
change the selection before the test ends. 
- Submits your answer into the system. Once you submit, 
you will not be able to change your answer.

PREPARING FOR THE PROCEDURES VALIDATION (PV)
The ERJ Procedures curriculum has been developed to provide an overview of gate-to-gate ground operations. There are
a total of 9 sessions, which includes a validation event. The first four sessions will be a 3-hour session on a matrix device, 
and a 3-hour instructor-led Ground Operation course in an FMS computer lab. During this week, we will provide guided 
procedures for ground operations only as we will initially omit any flying operations.
The objective of Procedures Basic is to train the students per the SOPM and FOM in the following:
• Normal Ground Tasks
• Normal Ground Flows
• Normal Ground Checklists
• Normal Checklist and Procedural Callouts
• MCDU Programming and Use
The ERJ Procedures Advanced course will be four 6-hour sessions (2-hour brief, 4-hour simulator session) in an Enhanced 
Visual Procedures Training Device (EVPT) and will build on the Procedures Basic Training to now add flight operations 
per the SOPM. On the 8th day of Procedures Training, the goal is to be recommended to take the Procedures Validation, 
which means all ground items mentioned above are meeting the program standards.
The Procedures Validation (PV) will be a 6-hour session (2-hour brief, 4-hour simulator session) in an Enhanced Visual 
Procedures Training Device (EVPT). Each student will take the PV separately and the evaluator will serve as the seat-sub 
in the appropriate position. The PV will last 1 hour for each student and will only validate the ground operations (no 
flying). The second half of the 4-hour simulator session will include special training (SPOT) and will not be a graded event 
on the PV. This training will be completed with your simulator partner and could include approaches, emergencies, and 
other events that will take place during Maneuvers Training (MT).
All standards and procedures are trained and evaluated per the ERJ SOPM SP3700 and should always include the most 
updated information.

PREPARING FOR THE MANEUVERS VALIDATION (MV)
Following Procedures Training, you will move ahead to the Maneuvers Training (MT) phase of the curriculum. A total of 
8 MT sessions are scheduled including a validation event. Each MT session is scheduled for 6 hours (2-hour brief, 4-hour 
simulator session) in a Level D full motion flight simulator. Note, there is only a 30-minute briefing preceding the 
Maneuvers Validation.
The objective of MT is to train the students per the SOPM and FOM in the following:
• Normal Flight Procedures
o Takeoff and Landing
o Instrument Approach Procedures
o Extended Envelope Training (EET)
• Abnormal/Emergency Flight Procedures
o Single Engine Procedures
o Emergency Descent
o Threat and Error Management
The Maneuvers Validation (MV) is conducted at the conclusion of your maneuvers training and prior to Line Oriented 
Flight Training (LOFT) and will be administered by a SkyWest Simulator Check Airman (SCA). The SCA will maintain a 
realistic environment to evaluate the required tasks outlined in the PAQPM. The SCA will provide appropriate 
communication throughout the MV as ATC, flight attendants, dispatch and maintenance. The SCA will avoid other 
communication with the student and is not allowed to teach or instruct during the MV. The rules and expectations of 
the MV will be briefed prior to the validation by the SCA.
The purpose of the MV is to validate proficiency in maneuvers and procedures that have been taught during maneuvers 
training. Reviewing the MT briefings and SOPM can be utilized in preparation for the MV. Applicants will be evaluated as 
Pilot Flying (PF) and Pilot Monitoring (PM) during the allotted 4-hour simulator session per the SOPM SP3700 .

PREPARING FOR THE LINE OPERATIONAL EVALUATION (LOE)
Line Oriented Flight Training (LOFT) follows the MT phase of the training curriculum and is the capstone phase of ground 
and simulator training. LOFT collectively ensures adequate knowledge of aircraft systems, SOPM procedures, and 
appropriate maneuvers for each student in a gate-to-gate environment prior to transitioning to line operations in the 
actual aircraft. LOFT contains four 6-hour simulator sessions (2-hour briefing, 4-hour simulator session) including an 
evaluation event in a level D full motion flight simulator. Each phase of training is purposefully organized to be built 
upon the next in preparation for this final phase. 
The Line Operational Evaluation (LOE) is conducted at the conclusion of LOFT training and the student has completed 
the SV, PV, MV, and KV successfully. The LOE will be conducted in a Level D full motion simulator and the applicant must 
treat it as an actual aircraft. The LOE will be administered by a SkyWest APD who will maintain a realistic environment 
conducive to evaluating the required events defined by the SkyWest Airlines Pilot Advanced Qualification Program 
Manual (PAQPM). The APD will provide appropriate communication throughout the LOE as ATC, flight attendants, 
dispatch, maintenance, and pushback crew. The APD will avoid other communication with the student and is not 
allowed to teach or instruct during the LOE. The rules and expectations of the LOE will be briefed prior to the validation 
by the APD.
The purpose of the LOE is to evaluate that the student has the required job knowledge, technical skill, operational 
proficiency and systems understanding to be a professional SkyWest pilot. The LOE will consist of two flight segments: 
One segment as Pilot Flying (PF), and one segment as Pilot monitoring (PM). Both segments will be graded, and 
applicants will need to show proficiency in tasks outlined in the SOPM and FOM. In preparation for the LOE you should 
review the topics discussed during LOFT training and incorporate skills and knowledge previously demonstrated 
throughout the training process. Review of tasks, flows, and profiles as outlined in the SOPM will be essential to 
demonstrate proficiency. The LOE is the primary method we use to evaluate a student’s ability to safely operate an 
aircraft for SkyWest Airlines and to receive a type rating in the appropriate aircraft.

PREPARING FOR THE KNOWLEDGE VALIDATION (KV)
The qualification Knowledge Validation (KV) is conducted prior to your qualification LOE as a part of your LOE prebriefing. The KV will be administered by your SkyWest Aircrew Program Designee (APD). 
This study guide helps aid in your preparation for the qualification KV. It’s important to acknowledge all topics covered 
in the qualification KV have been trained items at some point in the curriculum. Often, KV questions are directly sourced 
from our training slides. The purpose of this study guide is not to short-cut, diminish, or circumvent the training you’ve 
received, but rather, to add an additional resource. 
The objective of a KV is defined by the SkyWest Airlines Pilot Advanced Qualification Program Manual:
In an effort to maintain objectivity, the evaluator will draw from a common question base. The KV serves not only as 
validation of knowledge but could also be a learning experience as well. At the conclusion of the KV, the examiner will 
review incorrect answers and ensure students understand all subject matters questioned. The applicant may reference 
documentation available to them during line operations (EFB resources). Students are encouraged to find the correct 
answer rather than guess.
If the evaluator determines the student has not demonstrated satisfactory knowledge, additional questions consistent 
with the Qualification Standard may be used to determine a candidate’s overall knowledge.
Your knowledge will be validated in the following tasks during the qualification KV.
• Deice/Anti-ice Procedures
• Fuel Planning/Performance
• Memory Items

PURPOSE
This guide is designed to provide a structured technique on how to build the MCDU. Reference the most current SOPM revision for the specific Before Start tasks

BEFORE START TASKS (SOPM Ch. 4, Section 2)
We have organized the Before Start tasks from the SOPM into a memory aid: “ABC-123”. The ABC portion references ATIS/Activate, Build and Verify, Bug and

three pieces of data are as follows:
1) Weather for where we are at (D-ATIS, Voice ATIS, AWOS, ASOS)
2) Weather for where we are going (D-ATIS, METAR)
3) The clearance (PDC or clearance delivery)

“ABC-123”: TECHNIQUE WALKTHROUGH
“123”
Before we dive into building the MCDU, let’s gather the three pieces of data and make sure that we have Activated in FliteView (Completed the Originating 
Flow).
1. ATIS (Origin): Request the D-ATIS through ACARS if available. If not, tune in the radio frequency to listen to it on Comm 2. Once received, set the 
altimeter.
2. ATIS (Destination): Request the D-ATIS through ACARS if available. If not, request the METAR through ACARS. Crews are required to obtain the most upto-date destination weather information if the flight time is less than 1-hour to determine if they can depart (Main body weather above lowest approach 
mins).
3. Clearance: If you are at an airfield that has PDC, it will populate automatically through ACARS 30 minutes prior to departure. If a field is not serviced by 
PDC, tune in the appropriate frequency (Clearance Delivery, Ground, Tower, Center as required) to request your clearance. Generally, we don’t want to 
build the route in the MCDU until we have the cleared routing.

VERIFY
Now that we have finished the Build portion, let’s Verify our work. This has three steps:
1. Both pilots independently scroll through the waypoints on the MFD Plan page. The intent is to be zoomed out so you can get a 30,000’, birds-eye view to 
see if the route looks correct and all the “dots” are connected that we want connected. If there is a big gap, that means there is a discontinuity, and we 
should investigate to see if we want that. 
2. Crews will go through the PDC/Clearance together. One will read the clearance in its entirety while the other consults the appropriate MCDU pages and 
flight instruments to verify the information is set-up. This generally includes the Radio and RTE pages as well as the Altitude Preselect on the PFD.
3. Crews will verify all waypoint names, altitudes restrictions, and speed restrictions against the applicable Jeppesen charts to the TOD. The philosophy 
here is that the Pilot Flying (PF) owns the MCDU. The PF builds the route, and they will remain in the MCDU during the verification stage as well. Pilot 
Monitoring (PM) will read off the applicable charts to ensure that the data is correct.

ATIS: Get it, read it. May have to tune in and listen if the destination airport does not have D-ATIS.

PM reads STAR, Approach, and Missed Approach fixes and restrictions while PF verifies in the MCDU.
Bug: Use the acronym FCM (Frequency, Course, Minimums). Frequency: Verify appropriate frequency is set for ILS or LOC approach. Verify the WAAS channel 
and approach ID on an RNAV approach as applicable. This can be done on NAV > ARRIVAL. Course: Verify appropriate inbound course displayed with preview 
needle on HSI for any ground-based NAVAID approach. Verify preview needle is not displayed for an RNAV approach and the appropriate inbound course in the 
FMS. Minimums: Set appropriate minimums in the selector on the PFD. 
Also, verify the appropriate navigation source is selected (i.e. FMS 1 on CA side, FMS 2 on FO side).
Data: PM will send for/calculate Landing Data and enter it on PERF > Landing. Landing data can be requested from Aerodata through the DLK menu or by 
manually calculating it in the QRH performance section. In an emergency/abnormal situation, the QRH procedure may direct you to calculate performance with 
an included chart. PF sets desired Autobrake setting for landing/verifies speeds.
Calculate Vap as necessary by using the formula [Vap = Vref + ½ steady state HW comp + gust spread]. Verify it falls within Vref+5 to Vref+20 for No SPIS and 
Vref+0 to Vref+20 for SPIS/Cat II/Vref New. Vap must be calculated from scratch whenever there is a gust.
Brief: PF accomplishes the arrival WAANT briefing or delegates if workload requires. Reference the SOPM for examples.
Checklist and Flow: The Descent Checklist will be called by the PF once all tasks have been completed. If tasks are done prior to descending through 18,000’, the 
Descent Checklist will be called at 18,000’, both pilots will do their Descent flows, and the PM will run the Descent Checklist. If all tasks have not been completed 
prior to descending through 18,000’, both pilots will pause and do their Descent flows. Complete the remaining tasks then PF will call for the Descent Checklist.
