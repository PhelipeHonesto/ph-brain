**How to Use This Document**

This is the operational layer of OSA Connect. It does not replace the
current OSA Execution Memo --- it builds directly on it. The Memo is
strategy. **This is execution.**

Every tool is designed for a single founder operating part-time, at zero
capital. Nothing here requires staff, software subscriptions, or
complexity. Everything is designed to be copied, printed, or adapted
into whatever system you actually use.

The tools are organized in the sequence of a live trip booking:

1\. Inquiry Intake --- capturing the lead correctly the first time

2\. Client Qualification --- deciding whether and how to proceed

3\. Operator Vetting --- confirming the operator is bookable

4\. Quote Comparison --- evaluating options objectively

5\. Trip Workflow SOP --- the end-to-end booking process

6\. Pre-Departure Brief --- the client communication before every flight

7\. Post-Trip Follow-Up --- closing the loop and building the
relationship

8\. Referral Process --- turning good clients into deal flow

9\. Minimum CRM Structure --- tracking everything without a CRM
subscription

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **NOTE:** *Use these as living documents. Adapt language to your voice.
  Add fields you find useful. Remove anything that does not serve you.
  The only rule is consistency --- run the same process every time.*

**Tool 3 --- Operator Vetting Checklist**

**Purpose:** Confirm an operator is bookable before you put them in
front of a client. This checklist is completed once per operator and
updated when you encounter new information.

Operators who pass this checklist go on your Active Operator Roster.
Operators not yet vetted go on Pending. Operators who fail go on Do Not
Use with a written reason.

**Section A --- Regulatory & Safety Baseline**

• FAA Part 135 certificate confirmed --- verify on AIRS or request
certificate number directly

• Certificate is current --- not expired, surrendered, or suspended

• ARG/US or Wyvern safety rating exists (Platinum preferred; Gold
acceptable; unrated --- proceed with documented caution)

• No NTSB accidents in the last 5 years (search NTSB database by
operator name)

• Valid air carrier insurance confirmed --- minimum \$100M liability for
large jets; request certificate

• Aircraft on their operating certificate matches what they are quoting

**Section B --- Aircraft Condition**

• Aircraft year, make, model confirmed and matches the quote

• Interior photos available or personally viewed --- not stock photos

• Aircraft is based or positioned at the stated departure airport --- no
surprise repositioning fees

• Aircraft is not a dry-lease arrangement --- you need a wet lease with
crew included

• Any known squawks or deferred maintenance items disclosed? (Ask
directly. Note the answer.)

**Section C --- Operational Reliability**

• Direct contact established with a named dispatcher or scheduling
contact

• Quote response time: under 4 hours is excellent; same business day is
acceptable; next day is a yellow flag

• Quote is itemized: base charter rate, fuel surcharge, landing/ramp
fees, catering, FBO fees all visible

• Cancellation and substitution policy disclosed in writing before
booking

• If substitution is allowed --- what aircraft types are approved
substitutes?

• Personal reference or firsthand usage by a trusted contact (not
required, but log it if present)

**Section D --- Commercial Relationship**

• Broker commission structure confirmed and documented. Note:
operator-stated commission structures vary; OSA's realized commission
rate for financial planning purposes is modeled at 7% of gross bookings
(see Execution Memo financial scenarios). Confirm and document the
specific commission terms for each operator relationship.

• Payment terms agreed (standard: 25--50% deposit to confirm, balance
7--14 days before departure)

• Charter agreement template reviewed --- no unusual liability transfer
to the broker

• Operator has received and acknowledged your standard booking terms

**Vetting Outcome**

  -------------------------- --------------------------------------------
  **Status**                 **Criteria**

  **APPROVED --- Active      All Section A items checked. Majority of B,
  Roster**                   C, D checked. Named contact established.
                             Ready to quote.

  **CONDITIONAL ---          Section A complete. B/C/D gaps exist. Can
  Pending**                  quote but document the gaps. Resolve before
                             second booking.

  **DO NOT USE**             Any Section A failure. NTSB incident without
                             satisfactory explanation. Certificate
                             mismatch. Log the reason.
  -------------------------- --------------------------------------------

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **NOTE:** *Your operator vetting file is a competitive asset. Over time
  it becomes your most valuable operational tool. Keep it organized.
  Update it after every trip.*

**Tool 5 --- Trip Workflow SOP**

**Purpose:** A consistent, repeatable sequence for every booking from
inquiry to post-trip. Run this process identically every time --- no
exceptions. Consistency is how a one-person operation earns the
reputation of a larger company.

**Phase 1 --- Inquiry to Quote (Target: Within 24 Hours)**

  --------- ---------------------------------------------------------------
  **1.1**   Receive inquiry. Complete Tool 1 (Inquiry Intake) immediately
            or within 15 minutes.

  **1.2**   Acknowledge to client within 2 hours. Confirm routing, dates,
            PAX count. Set a specific quote delivery time --- not 'as soon
            as possible.'

  **1.3**   Run Tool 2 (Client Qualification). If Proceed, continue. If
            Defer or Decline, follow those protocols.

  **1.4**   Identify 2--3 candidate operators from Active Roster. If
            routing requires an unvetted operator, complete Tool 3 first.

  **1.5**   Contact operators. Send trip spec clearly: routing, dates, PAX,
            special requirements. Request itemized quote.

  **1.6**   Complete Tool 4 (Quote Comparison) when 2+ quotes received.
            Identify recommendation.

  **1.7**   Prepare client quote: recommended aircraft, all-in price, key
            terms, brief recommendation. One page or equivalent.

  **1.8**   Deliver quote to client. If by phone, follow up with written
            summary within one hour.
  --------- ---------------------------------------------------------------

**Phase 2 --- Quote to Booking Confirmation (Target: Same Day as
Acceptance)**

  --------- ---------------------------------------------------------------
  **2.1**   Client accepts quote verbally or in writing. Confirm
            immediately in writing: 'Confirmed --- here is what we are
            booking\...'

  **2.2**   Send charter agreement to client. Review for unusual liability
            clauses before forwarding.

  **2.3**   Collect deposit per operator payment terms. Confirm receipt in
            writing.

  **2.4**   Confirm booking with operator. Get written confirmation of:
            aircraft, tail number, crew names, departure time, FBO
            location.

  **2.5**   Log booking in CRM (Tool 9). Record: operator, aircraft, tail
            number, PAX names, contact, amounts, payment status.

  **2.6**   Set calendar reminders: pre-departure brief (48--72 hours
            pre-departure) and post-trip follow-up (24 hours after
            landing).
  --------- ---------------------------------------------------------------

**Phase 3 --- Confirmed Booking to Departure (Ongoing)**

  --------- ---------------------------------------------------------------
  **3.1**   Collect balance from client per agreement terms. Confirm
            receipt with operator.

  **3.2**   Collect passenger names and dates of birth. Forward to operator
            per their requirements (often required 72 hours out).

  **3.3**   48--72 hours before departure: deliver Pre-Departure Brief
            (Tool 6).

  **3.4**   Day of departure: text or call client to confirm. 'Everything
            is confirmed and ready --- your crew is \[first name\] and
            \[first name\].'

  **3.5**   After departure: monitor. If any disruption occurs, you are the
            first call from the client. Have the operator's dispatch line
            ready.
  --------- ---------------------------------------------------------------

**Phase 4 --- Post-Trip (Within 24 Hours of Landing)**

  --------- ---------------------------------------------------------------
  **4.1**   Send post-trip follow-up (Tool 7) within 24 hours of scheduled
            landing.

  **4.2**   Update CRM with trip outcome: actual vs. scheduled, any issues,
            client satisfaction signal, referral potential.

  **4.3**   If operator issues occurred: document in operator file. If
            serious, flag as Conditional or Do Not Use.

  **4.4**   If client satisfaction is high: begin referral process (Tool 8)
            within 5 business days.

  **4.5**   Set next follow-up date in CRM. Minimum 30 days. For frequent
            travelers: 15 days.
  --------- ---------------------------------------------------------------

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **NOTE:** *The most common failure point is Steps 4.1--4.5. Most
  brokers disappear after the trip. Showing up afterward is the primary
  way OSA Connect earns a different reputation.*

**Tool 7 --- Post-Trip Follow-Up Template**

**Purpose:** Close every trip with a professional, genuine follow-up
that reinforces the relationship, surfaces any issues before they become
complaints, and plants the seed for repeat business. This is the most
underused tool in charter brokerage.

**Timing:** Within 24 hours of scheduled landing. For same-day trips,
send the evening of or the morning after. Do not wait longer than 24
hours.

**Delivery:** Text or email depending on client preference. Keep it
brief and human --- this is not a survey.

**Tool 8 --- Referral Process**

**Purpose:** Convert satisfied clients into a reliable source of new
business. Referrals are the highest-quality leads in private aviation
--- they arrive with pre-built trust. This process creates a consistent
mechanism for earning them.

**When to Activate**

  ------------------------------ ----------------------------------------
  **Condition**                  **Action**

  **Client satisfaction is high  Proceed with referral seed (Template C
  after a completed trip**       from Tool 7)

  **Client has booked 2+ times** Direct referral ask is appropriate

  **Client mentions a colleague  Follow up with a specific, low-pressure
  or contact who charters**      offer

  **Client has never indicated   Do not ask for referrals. Earn the

structured, consistent way. This is the minimum viable CRM --- four
tables that can be built in any spreadsheet, Notion database, or CRM
platform.

The CRM is not about the tool. It is about the discipline. A Google
Sheet updated daily is more valuable than a \$200/month CRM updated
monthly.

**CRM Maintenance Protocol**

• Update after every client contact --- same day, not later.

• Review Pipeline (Table 4) every Monday morning. Move leads to Lost if
no response in 14 days.

• Update operator performance (Table 3) within 48 hours of every
completed trip.

• Monthly review of Contact Master (Table 1) --- look for dormant
relationships worth re-engaging.

• Add every new contact the day you meet them, even if they are not yet
a client.

  **NOTE:** *Your CRM is also your audit trail if any dispute arises.
  Keep notes accurate and factual. Do not write anything you would not
  want a client to read.*

**Client Communication Standards**

• Always confirm routing, dates, and passenger count before quoting.

• Never promise an aircraft you have not confirmed with the operator.

• Never give a price verbally without following up in writing.

• If something changes after confirmation --- aircraft, timing, crew ---
tell the client before they find out another way.

• If you do not know the answer to a question, say so. 'I don't have
that detail --- let me confirm and come back within an hour' is more
credible than a guess.

• Never disparage an operator to a client. Address issues privately and
directly with the operator.
