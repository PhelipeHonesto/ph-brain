**Platform Evidence Base**

These cadence decisions are grounded in current platform data, not
assumptions. The evidence is summarized here so the reasoning is
transparent and auditable. Platform algorithms change frequently; treat
these as directional inputs, not permanent truths. Full source list in
Appendix B.

**LinkedIn**

LinkedIn's VP of Product Management Gyanda Sachdeva stated in December
2025 that users should aim for 2--5 LinkedIn posts per week for optimal
impact, noting that members posting at least twice per week tend to see
significantly more profile views. Buffer's analysis of over 2 million
posts from 94,000+ accounts found that moving from 1 post per week into
the 2--5 range corresponds with meaningful gains in both per-post reach
and engagement. Multiple 2026 practitioner analyses converge on 3--5
posts per week as the recommended range for founder-led B2B accounts.
Current data also points toward a content cannibalization effect when
posting more than once per day --- practitioner analyses indicate the
algorithm rarely shows two posts from the same creator to the same user
within a short window, which can reduce per-post distribution.

At a target of 4 posts per week with a minimum 24-hour buffer between
posts, Loud Hawk sits in the upper band of the recommended range without
triggering self-competition. This is aggressive enough to build
algorithmic momentum but disciplined enough to let each post breathe.

One additional signal worth noting: multiple 2025 studies and
practitioner reports indicate that posts containing external links in
the body tend to see materially lower reach than native-format posts.
This reinforces the existing Loud Hawk practice of placing subscribe
links in the first comment rather than the post body, and writing
LinkedIn content that stands alone as native value.

**Instagram**

Adam Mosseri, head of Instagram, has stated publicly that posting more
generally leads to faster follower growth because more content gets
discovered and shared. Buffer's analysis of 2 million+ Instagram posts
similarly found a positive relationship between posting frequency and
follower growth, recommending 3--5 feed posts per week as the practical
range where effort and returns tend to balance.

Socialinsider's 2026 Instagram benchmarks report two format trends
directly relevant to Loud Hawk's strategy:

-   Static images are declining in engagement and in posting volume,
    with Socialinsider reporting a year-over-year decrease of
    approximately 17%, reflecting reduced impact across the platform.

-   Carousels are driving the most saves and views across all brand
    sizes, with Socialinsider reporting engagement rates holding steady
    at approximately 0.55% --- outperforming both Reels and static
    images in their dataset.

This data supports evolving the Instagram strategy toward more carousels
alongside the existing news pill static format. The current target of 4
posts per week accommodates a mix of static news pills, carousels, and
market observation posts. As carousel production becomes systematized
(via the planned Figma template + plugin workflow), the ratio should
shift toward more carousels and fewer static images.

Instagram's algorithm in 2025--2026 ranks content based on three primary
signals confirmed by Mosseri: **watch time, likes per reach, and sends
(DM shares) per reach**. Practitioner analyses indicate that DM shares
are weighted substantially higher than likes for reaching new audiences.
Carousels naturally perform well on the dwell-time signal because users
spend more time swiping through multiple slides, and benchmarks suggest
they generate more saves than other formats. For Loud Hawk's niche
intelligence content, carousels are the format best aligned to the
algorithm's reported priorities.

**2. Funnel Architecture**

Every piece of Loud Hawk and OSA content serves a position in the
marketing funnel. Understanding which stage a piece of content occupies
determines its format, its tone, its platform, and which brand leads.
Content that mixes funnel stages within a single piece dilutes its
impact. Be clear about what each piece is doing.

**TOFU --- Top of Funnel: Attract Attention**

**Definition:** Content that reaches people who don't know Loud Hawk or
OSA yet. The goal is not conversion --- it's recognition. The reader's
internal response should be: "Who is this person?"

**Best formats for the aviation niche:** Rankings and tier lists
(aircraft categories, charter options), myth-busting and Gap-style
corrections, market signal snapshots, quick takes on industry news,
Q&A-style explainers, "what this means" observations on market events.

**Brand ownership:** Loud Hawk leads. OSA parent brand supports as the
entity behind the voice.

**Platform distribution:** LinkedIn forward-testing posts, Instagram
news pills and carousels, future Reels (when strategy is ready).

**TOFU Content Examples**

-   \"Light jet charter pricing is up 18% since January. Here's what's
    driving it.\" (MS, LinkedIn)

-   \"Most people think charter pricing is fixed. It isn't. Here's what
    actually moves the number.\" (TG, LinkedIn)

-   Instagram news pill: FAA issues new SMS guidance for Part 135
    operators (SA, IG static)

-   \"Tier list: when jet cards actually make sense vs. when they
    don't\" (TG, IG carousel)

-   \"The operator that just lost its ARG/US rating --- and what it
    means for your next quote\" (SA, LinkedIn)

**MOFU --- Middle of Funnel: Build Trust and Preference**

**Definition:** Content that converts attention into trust. The reader
already knows who you are. Now they're evaluating whether you actually
know what you're talking about. The reader's internal response should
be: "This person knows the game."

**Best formats for the aviation niche:** How-it-actually-works process
explainers, step-by-step breakdowns (how a charter quote is built, how
aircraft sourcing works), mistake-avoidance frameworks, in-depth
education, storytelling from real market experience, newsletter
editions, multi-slide carousels.

**Brand ownership:** Loud Hawk and OSA Connect share this space. Loud
Hawk provides the editorial depth. OSA Connect begins to appear as the
entity behind the expertise.

**Platform distribution:** LinkedIn insight and process posts, Instagram
carousels, newsletter editions.

**MOFU Content Examples**

-   \"How charter operators actually price a trip --- why the same route
    is \$22K on Tuesday and \$34K on Thursday\" (HIAW, newsletter main
    piece)

-   \"What happens between the quote and the wheels-up --- the 9 steps
    nobody explains\" (HIAW, IG carousel)

-   \"The 3 mistakes first-time charter clients make that cost them
    money\" (TG, LinkedIn)

-   \"Repositioning legs: when they're a deal and when they're a trap\"
    (HIAW, LinkedIn)

-   Newsletter deep-dive on seasonal demand patterns and what they mean
    for Q4 booking (MS, newsletter)

**BOFU --- Bottom of Funnel: Create Conversion Confidence**

**Definition:** Content that creates enough confidence for someone to
reach out. The reader already trusts your knowledge. Now they need to
believe you can execute for them specifically. The reader's internal
response should be: "I'd trust them with my trip or my transaction."

**Best formats for the aviation niche:** Scenario breakdowns ("if this
were the brief, here's how we'd approach it"), mission simulations,
objection handling, service process explainers, before-and-after process
narratives. Important: these are not fake case studies. OSA has not
earned the right to claim a deep track record. Use consultative advisory
language that demonstrates thinking, not fabricated client stories.

**Brand ownership:** OSA Connect and OSA Aircraft lead. Loud Hawk
assists by providing the credibility that got the reader here.

**Platform distribution:** LinkedIn (selective --- not every post),
newsletter (when appropriate), OSA website.

**BOFU Content Examples**

-   \"Scenario: 6 passengers, TETERBORO to BOZEMAN, peak summer. Here's
    how we'd source it and what to watch for.\" (LinkedIn, occasional)

-   \"What a good charter broker does that you'll never see --- the work
    between the quote and the flight\" (HIAW/BOFU crossover, newsletter)

-   \"You got two quotes. One is \$24K, one is \$31K. Here's why the
    cheaper one might cost you more.\" (TG/BOFU crossover, LinkedIn)

-   OSA Connect service page: \"How we work --- from brief to
    wheels-up\" (OSA website)

-   \"Before you buy an aircraft: the 5 questions your broker should be
    asking you\" (OSA Aircraft advisory, LinkedIn)

**The Prospect Flow**

The ecosystem is designed so a prospect moves through a natural sequence
from discovery to engagement to conversion, with each brand handing off
to the next at the right moment.

**Discovery:** A prospect encounters a Loud Hawk LinkedIn post or
Instagram news pill --- a sharp market observation, a myth-busting take,
a pricing signal. They think: "Who is this?" They tap through to the
profile.

**Follow and subscribe:** The prospect follows on LinkedIn or Instagram.
If the content is strong enough, they subscribe to Loud Hawk on Beehiiv.
They begin receiving biweekly editions. Trust accumulates over 2--4
editions as the content proves genuine market knowledge.

**OSA becomes familiar:** The prospect sees occasional OSA Connect or
OSA Aircraft references --- a soft CTA at the bottom of a newsletter, a
scenario breakdown on LinkedIn, the OSA website link in the bio. They
absorb these passively. OSA becomes a known entity, not a cold outreach.

**Conversion trigger:** When the prospect needs a charter or has an
aircraft question, OSA feels familiar and low-risk. The outreach is warm
because the trust was built before the need arose. This is the entire
point of the content system.

The critical design principle: Loud Hawk never feels like a dressed-up
sales funnel for OSA. It earns attention through genuine editorial
value. The commercial connection to OSA is quiet, consistent, and never
the reason someone opens the email or reads the post.

**The Carousel Opportunity**

Current platform data makes a strong case for increasing carousel
production as a strategic priority. Socialinsider's 2026 Instagram
benchmarks report carousels as the highest-engagement format on the
platform, outperforming both Reels and static images. Benchmarks also
suggest carousels drive the most saves --- a metric Instagram's
algorithm is reported to value highly for signaling content quality.

For Loud Hawk's niche intelligence content, carousels are an ideal
format: they allow multi-slide breakdowns of market data, step-by-step
process explanations, and newsletter repurposing in a format that
rewards dwell time and rewards the reader's attention with progressively
deeper insight.

The current constraint is production systemization. The lh-news-pill
skill handles static posts; carousel production requires a separate
workflow (Figma template + plugin for systematized visual production).
Until that workflow is operational, carousels are produced manually at a
lower frequency. As the system comes online, the IG content mix should
shift toward approximately 40--50% carousels, 30--40% news pills, and
10--20% market observation statics. Reels will layer on top once
strategy is ready.

**7. Editorial Guardrails**

The production skills changed the capacity equation. They did not change
the editorial standards. These guardrails govern every piece of content
regardless of how it was produced.

**Quality Control**

**The kill switch exists for a reason.** Both the lh-linkedin and
lh-news-pill skills include editorial judgment layers --- kill switches,
hold logic, quality checks. If a post doesn't clear the quality bar, it
doesn't publish. Three strong posts beat four mediocre ones. Never
publish a post just to hit a number. This is why the cadence system
distinguishes between a target cadence and a publish floor.

**Off-week LinkedIn posts must stand alone.** No newsletter links in
off-week posts. The insight is the product. If someone finds your
profile and subscribes, the system worked. If you have to direct them,
the content wasn't strong enough. This rule is non-negotiable.

**Every main claim gets internally tagged.** Directly observed, inferred
from repeated observations, sourced from public reporting, or
opinion/interpretation. This tag is for internal use --- it never
appears in published content. It keeps the writing honest about what you
know versus what you're interpreting.

**Platform Discipline**

**Don't cross-post formats.** The same observation can appear on both
LinkedIn and Instagram, but the format must be platform-native. LinkedIn
gets text paragraphs. Instagram gets a visual card. Never screenshot a
LinkedIn post and post it on Instagram. Never copy an IG caption into a
LinkedIn post.

**Don't cross-post the entire newsletter to any social platform.** The
newsletter is the product. Social content creates interest and drives
subscriptions. It does not replace the email.

**Intellectual Honesty**

**The newsletter remains the credential test.** "Would I send this to a
prospective OSA client?" still applies to every edition. Social content
can be slightly more informal, but it still carries the Loud Hawk name.

**Do not fabricate specifics to fill a post.** If you don't have a
specific data point, write at the appropriate level of generality. An
honest observation stated clearly is always better than a fabricated
number that sounds impressive.

**Match certainty to evidence.** No posturing beyond earned expertise.
The Execution Memo is clear: do not publish opinion on topics where
genuine expertise has not yet been earned.

**AI skills accelerate production, but real market observation is still
the product.** The skills are production tools. The value proposition is
the founder's market knowledge, operator relationships, and direct
observation. If the observation pipeline dries up, the skills can't fix
it.

**9. Decision Gates**

Review this cadence at the end of every 4 cycles (8 weeks). The
questions below are designed to generate binary decisions, not
open-ended reflection. Answer them with data, then act.

**Cadence Review (8-Week Gate)**

**Are you hitting target cadence on LinkedIn consistently?** If you're
regularly publishing only at the floor (3/week), that's operationally
fine --- but investigate whether the constraint is observation input,
editorial selectivity, or time. If it's observation input, the content
system needs more market exposure, not more production capacity.

**Are you hitting target cadence on Instagram consistently?** If the
news pill pipeline is flowing, this should be sustainable. If you're
regularly at the floor, evaluate whether carousel production coming
online would fill the gap or whether the issue is source material.

**Is engagement growing or flat?** Growing means the cadence is working.
Flat means the content mix needs adjustment, not the frequency. Diagnose
which lane or format is underperforming before changing the number of
posts.

**Is the forward-testing pipeline feeding the newsletter?** The
best-performing standalone LinkedIn posts should naturally become
newsletter material. If this loop isn't forming, the forward-testing
content may not be sharp enough, or the newsletter editorial process
isn't pulling from social signals.

**Is Saturday LinkedIn performing?** Compare Saturday post impressions
and engagement to the Tuesday--Thursday average. If Saturday
consistently underperforms by a meaningful margin after 4 cycles, move
to a different weekday (Monday is the natural replacement) or drop to 3
posts per week.

**Absolute Constraints**

**Do not increase cadence beyond these numbers in this phase.** Daily or
near-daily LinkedIn posting is a future state that requires a proven
audience, deeper content pipeline, and potentially additional team
capacity. That state has not been reached. Four posts per week with high
editorial standards is the right ceiling for the next 90 days.

**Do not expand beyond LinkedIn, Instagram, and the newsletter.** In
this updated content phase, do not expand beyond LinkedIn, Instagram,
and the newsletter until the current system proves itself through at
least 4 full cycles of consistent execution and measurable traction.

**Do not sacrifice quality for consistency.** If a week has only 3
publishable observations for LinkedIn, publish 3. The kill switch is
more important than the calendar. Loud Hawk's edge is being selective
and sharp, not being everywhere all the time.

lane for tracking, balance review, and editorial planning. These codes

  **Code**   **Lane Name** **Definition**

  TG         The Gap       The distance between how the private aviation
                           industry markets itself and how it actually
                           operates. Covers: industry marketing vs.
                           operational reality, client misconceptions the
                           industry benefits from maintaining, concierge
                           promises vs. actual service delivery, empty leg
                           marketing vs. empty leg truth. This is Loud Hawk's
                           sharpest editorial voice and the most shareable

  MS         Market Signal Observable movements in the private aviation
