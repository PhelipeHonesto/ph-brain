**FOREWORD**

**A Note on Realism**

*This document is a serious long-term strategy memo. It is not a pitch
deck, an investor teaser, or an optimistic projection of what OSA could
become in five years. It is a disciplined framework for building a
credible aviation business over 15 to 25 years --- starting with no
capital.*

Private aviation is a relationship-intensive, capital-intensive, and
cyclically sensitive industry. Participants who enter with unrealistic
expectations often exit quickly. The purpose of this roadmap is to
ensure the founder enters with clear eyes: understanding what the early
years will actually look like, what must be accumulated before capital
is deployed, and what conditions must be met before the business can
advance from one stage to the next.

The founder is an airline pilot --- a working First Officer on the
Embraer E175 at SkyWest Airlines, holding an ATP with type rating and
CFI/CFII/MEI certificates. That is a significant asset, not merely for
credibility, but because it means the business can be built part-time
during early years without depleting personal savings. The
system-building period that began in 2025 has produced operational
infrastructure: a CRM, charter execution workflows, a content production
system, and a live media presence through Loud Hawk. These systems
reduce administrative friction --- they do not replace the years of
market participation, relationship-building, and transaction-earned
credibility that private aviation requires.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  *OSA's most important early assets are not financial. They are
  knowledge, relationships, and reputation. These take years to develop
  and cannot be compressed with money or tools. This roadmap respects
  that reality.*

  -------- --------------------------------------------------------------
  **01**   **LONG-TERM VISION OF OSA**

  -------- --------------------------------------------------------------

*What OSA could realistically become over 15 to 25 years --- and how the
founding entities interact.*

The long-term vision for OSA is to become a credible, well-respected
aviation advisory and asset management company with roots in brokerage
and a meaningful presence in aircraft asset management. This is a
20-year ambition, not a 5-year plan.

The word 'platform' is often overused in startup contexts. For OSA, it
means something specific: a set of interconnected businesses where
knowledge generated in one operation benefits the others. Charter
brokerage generates market intelligence that informs aircraft brokerage.
Aircraft brokerage develops owner relationships that enable asset
management. Media strengthens visibility and credibility, with
commercial impact expected to develop gradually as audience and industry
engagement grow.

What OSA will not do is attempt to skip stages. Each stage requires
genuine expertise that cannot be faked, and attempting to offer asset
management or aviation investment services without the underlying
knowledge and track record will damage the brand in an industry where
reputation is everything.

**An Emergent Value Layer: Proprietary Systems**

In the course of building operational infrastructure, OSA has begun
developing proprietary production tools, workflow systems, and content
frameworks that are purpose-built for private aviation brokerage and
media operations. These are not a product and not a current strategy ---
they are a byproduct of building the business with a systems-first
discipline. Over time, if these tools prove durable and differentiated,
they may constitute intellectual property with standalone value: as
licensable systems, as a basis for future capital raising, or as
competitive moats that deepen with use. This is noted as a long-term
optionality, not a near-term priority. The current priority remains
building market capability through transactions and relationships.

**How the Three Entities Interact**

The three founding entities are designed to reinforce each other, but
this should not be overstated in early years. In Phase 1, each entity
will operate largely independently. Integration deepens as each develops
its own identity and capabilities.

**Brokerage as the Market Intelligence Engine**

Every charter booking and aircraft transaction exposes the founder to
data that is genuinely difficult to access from the outside. What do
clients actually pay for different cabin sizes and routes? How much does
it cost an operator to position an aircraft? What does an aircraft in a
given condition actually sell for versus list price?

This information is not available from public sources. It is earned
through transactions. After hundreds of bookings and a dozen or more
aircraft transactions, the founder will possess market knowledge that
most aviation finance professionals --- who approach the industry from a
purely financial perspective --- never develop. That knowledge is the
foundation on which later stages are built.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  *A credible aircraft asset manager must understand not just the
  financial structure of aviation assets, but the operational and market
  realities underneath them. Charter brokerage is the most efficient way
  to develop that understanding without deploying capital.*

  -------- --------------------------------------------------------------
  **02**   **REALISTIC MULTI-STAGE INDUSTRY PROGRESSION**

  -------- --------------------------------------------------------------

*The four stages of OSA's development --- and what each one actually
requires.*

The four-stage model is well-established in the aviation industry. Many
charter brokers never advance beyond Stage 1. A smaller number
successfully build aircraft brokerage businesses. Fewer still reach
Stage 3. Stage 4 --- aircraft leasing and aviation investment --- is
reached by very few, and only after decades of work and significant
capital accumulation.

This roadmap treats each stage as a genuine milestone, not a timeline
checkpoint. OSA should not advance to the next stage until the current
stage is generating consistent, reliable revenue and the knowledge
required for the next stage has been meaningfully developed.

  -------- --------------------------------------------------------------
  **01**   **Charter Brokerage --- OSA Connect**

  -------- --------------------------------------------------------------

*Entry point. Capital-light. Intelligence-generating.*

**Business Model**

OSA Connect acts as an intermediary between clients seeking private jet
transport and FAA Part 135 certificated operators who own and operate
aircraft. OSA does not own aircraft, hold aviation certificates, or bear
operational risk. The business is entirely relationship and knowledge
driven.

**Revenue Streams**

-   Commission on charter bookings, typically 5--10% of gross charter
    price

-   Occasional service fees for complex trip coordination

-   Over time: preferred pricing from high-volume operator relationships

**Operational Requirements**

-   Working knowledge of FAA Part 135 operations and aircraft categories

-   Relationships with vetted charter operators across multiple aircraft
    types

-   Access to operator availability and pricing databases

-   A CRM system for managing client and operator relationships

-   Clear client agreements and liability understanding

**Current Operational Status (March 2026)**

The OSA Connect Operating System is built and functional. This includes:
an inquiry intake form, client qualification flow, operator vetting
checklist, quote comparison template, trip workflow SOP, pre-departure
brief template, post-trip follow-up template, referral process, and a
minimum CRM structure with pipeline, contacts, quotes, operators, and
task tracking. These systems mean the business can execute a charter
booking professionally from day one. They do not mean the business has
proven it can attract, close, and retain clients at scale. That remains
the work of Phase 1.

**Knowledge Advantages Gained**

-   Real-world charter pricing across all cabin categories and routes

-   Operator reliability, capacity, and utilization patterns

-   How high-net-worth clients make aviation decisions

-   Charter demand patterns: seasonality, route preferences, lead times

**Typical Barriers to Entry**

-   No formal licensing required, but credibility is difficult to
    establish without a track record

-   Established brokers have existing operator relationships and client
    bases

-   New brokers must compete on service quality and responsiveness, not
    price

  -------- --------------------------------------------------------------
  **02**   **Aircraft Brokerage --- OSA Aircraft**

  -------- --------------------------------------------------------------

*Transaction expertise. Owner relationships. Valuation knowledge.*

**Business Model**

OSA Aircraft represents buyers and sellers of pre-owned business
aircraft. This naturally extends from charter brokerage, as high-value
charter clients frequently become aircraft buyers. The transition
requires a meaningful increase in product knowledge and transaction
expertise.

**Revenue Streams**

-   Seller representation: 2--5% commission on aircraft sale price

-   Buyer representation: Fee-based or co-commission with seller's
    broker

-   Acquisition advisory: Retainer-based research, inspection
    coordination, and structuring

-   Refurbishment project management fees (see Section 3 for detail)

**Operational Requirements**

-   Deep aircraft valuation knowledge across multiple types

-   Pre-purchase inspection coordination with qualified maintenance
    organizations

-   Understanding of aircraft title, escrow, and closing processes

-   Relationships with aviation attorneys, lenders, and tax advisors

-   Access to aircraft listing databases

**Knowledge Advantages Gained**

-   Aircraft depreciation curves, maintenance event timing, and value
    inflection points

-   True ownership economics: what aircraft actually cost to own and
    operate

-   Relationships with aircraft owners --- the most valuable network for
    later stages

-   Exposure to aircraft financing structures and leaseback economics

**Typical Barriers to Entry**

-   Aircraft transactions are high-stakes; clients require demonstrated
    expertise

-   Established aircraft brokers have deep manufacturer and operator
    relationships

-   Each transaction type (piston, turboprop, light jet, large cabin)
    has distinct complexity

  -------- --------------------------------------------------------------
  **03**   **Aircraft Asset Management**

  -------- --------------------------------------------------------------

*Fee-based services. No aircraft ownership required.*

**Business Model**

Having established relationships with aircraft owners through both
charter and aircraft brokerage, OSA begins offering asset management
services: advising on ownership structuring, managing charter revenue
programs, and coordinating maintenance and crew management on behalf of
owners who prefer not to handle these functions directly.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  *Stage 3 does not require OSA to own aircraft. It generates recurring
  fee income based on expertise and trust --- both of which must be
  earned through consistent performance in Stages 1 and 2.*

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

**Revenue Streams**

-   Monthly management advisory fees per aircraft

-   Charter revenue participation: a percentage of gross charter revenue
    from managed aircraft

-   Transaction fees on acquisitions and dispositions of managed
    aircraft

-   Consulting retainers for institutional aircraft owners or operators

**Operational Requirements**

-   Demonstrated track record from Stages 1 and 2

-   Software for aircraft maintenance tracking, scheduling, and
    financial reporting

-   Crew coordination capability or outsourced crew management
    relationships

-   Possible Part 135 operator relationships or certificate (significant
    regulatory step)

  -------- --------------------------------------------------------------
  **04**   **Aircraft Leasing & Aviation Investment**

  -------- --------------------------------------------------------------

*Capital deployment. Reached only after decades of expertise.*

**Business Model**

At Stage 4, OSA transitions from purely fee-based advisory to deploying
capital into aviation assets --- purchasing aircraft for leasing,
structuring aviation investment vehicles, or forming partnerships with
institutional capital providers. This stage should not be approached
before OSA has a genuine track record, substantial capital reserves, and
institutional relationships.

**Revenue Streams**

-   Net lease income on owned aircraft

-   Asset appreciation on aircraft acquired at market lows

-   Fund management fees and carried interest if aviation investment
    vehicles are formed

-   Co-investment arrangement income from institutional partners

**A Possible Future Vehicle: Aircraft Acquisition & Leasing Fund**

One potential structure for Stage 4 capital deployment is a dedicated
aircraft acquisition and leasing fund --- a pooled vehicle that acquires
business aircraft, places them on charter or lease, and distributes cash
flow to investors. This structure would combine OSA's transaction
expertise and operator relationships (earned over a decade or more of
brokerage) with institutional or family-office capital. The fund would
generate management fees on assets under management, performance fees on
returns, and direct cash flow from leased or charter-placed aircraft.

This is noted as one possible future vehicle, not a current plan. The
prerequisites are formidable: a verified multi-year track record of
profitable aircraft transactions, deep institutional relationships
(likely developed through the MBA and Phase 3 advisory work), regulatory
and legal infrastructure for fund formation, and market conditions that
support aircraft acquisition at attractive valuations. The decision to
pursue this structure --- or not --- belongs to a future version of OSA
that has earned the credibility to make it.

**Operational Requirements**

-   Significant capital base --- aircraft acquisition requires millions
    in equity or financed capital

-   Aviation finance expertise, including lien management and
    depreciation tax strategy

-   Legal infrastructure for SPV formation and investor reporting

-   Institutional relationships with aviation lenders and family office
    investors

**Typical Barriers to Entry**

-   Capital requirements are substantial; aircraft cost \$2M to \$80M or
    more

-   Aviation lenders require demonstrated aviation industry expertise
    and track record

-   Competition from established leasing companies with cost-of-capital
    advantages

-   Market cycles can destroy returns on poorly timed aircraft
    acquisitions

  **03**   **EXPANDED SCOPE OF AIRCRAFT BROKERAGE**

  -------- --------------------------------------------------------------

*Aircraft brokerage is broader than buying and selling. OSA Aircraft can
offer a range of advisory services that deepen expertise without
requiring additional capital.*

Many aircraft brokers limit themselves to listing and selling aircraft.
OSA Aircraft should develop a broader advisory capability that positions
it as a genuine aircraft lifecycle expert --- not merely a transaction
facilitator. This differentiation is important in a market where many
brokers compete on the same listings.

**Core Transaction Services**

**Seller Representation**

OSA Aircraft represents aircraft owners seeking to sell, preparing
market analyses, managing aircraft presentations and showings,
coordinating pre-purchase inspection access, and negotiating terms on
behalf of the seller.

**Buyer Representation**

OSA Aircraft identifies aircraft that meet a buyer's mission profile,
value requirements, and ownership objectives. This includes screening
available listings, sourcing off-market aircraft through operator and
owner relationships, and coordinating the due diligence process through
pre-purchase inspection and closing.

**Aircraft Sourcing**

Some buyers --- particularly those with specific equipment requirements
--- may engage OSA Aircraft on a retainer basis to identify aircraft
that are not actively listed for sale. Off-market sourcing requires deep
operator and owner relationships that develop over years of activity in
the charter and brokerage markets.

**Advisory and Lifecycle Services**

**Interior Refurbishment Advisory**

Aircraft interiors depreciate and become dated. Owners considering
selling --- or buyers who have acquired an aircraft in need of updating
--- often require guidance on refurbishment options, costs, and return
on investment. OSA Aircraft can develop expertise in advising on
interior refurbishment projects: what upgrades justify their cost at
sale, which completion centers offer the best value and quality, and how
refurbishment timing affects aircraft marketability.

**Cabin Upgrades and Completion Center Management**

For larger aircraft or more complex refurbishment projects, OSA Aircraft
can act as a project manager representing the owner's interests through
a completion center engagement. This involves specification development,
completion center selection, project oversight, and quality assurance.
This service requires familiarity with major completion centers, typical
project timelines, and common cost overrun scenarios.

**Acquisition-to-Disposition Lifecycle Advisory**

The most sophisticated version of OSA Aircraft's service offering is
advising an aircraft owner through the full ownership lifecycle: from
acquisition through configuration, maintenance management, charter
revenue strategy, and eventual disposition. This advisory relationship,
if managed well, can last many years and generate recurring fee income
across multiple transactions.

**How This Deepens Expertise**

  ---------------------- ------------------------------------------------
  **Service Area**       **Expertise Developed**

  Aircraft transactions  Valuation, negotiation, market dynamics,
                         depreciation curves

  Buyer representation   Mission analysis, aircraft selection criteria,
                         due diligence processes

  Off-market sourcing    Owner network depth, relationship trust, market
                         intelligence

  Refurbishment advisory Completion center knowledge, ROI analysis,
                         interior market preferences

  Lifecycle management   Full ownership economics, maintenance cost
                         modeling, exit timing strategy
  ---------------------- ------------------------------------------------

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  *Each service area OSA Aircraft develops is a layer of expertise that
  competitors without deep brokerage roots cannot easily replicate. The
  goal is to become the advisor that aircraft owners trust throughout
  ownership --- not just at the transaction points.*

acquisition with cash flow distribution into leasing. Both scenarios
require years of proven execution before they become credible. Capital
raising on the basis of systems alone --- without transaction revenue
and market credibility --- would be premature and strategically
misguided.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  *The most common mistake made by aviation entrepreneurs is deploying
  capital into aircraft before they understand aviation asset economics
  from the inside. Brokerage is the mechanism for developing that
  understanding safely, and the years spent in brokerage are not a delay
  --- they are the investment.*

  **07**   **STRATEGIC ADVANTAGES OF STARTING WITH BROKERAGE**

  -------- --------------------------------------------------------------

*Brokerage is not merely a low-capital entry point. It is the most
efficient mechanism for building the knowledge base and relationships
that private aviation requires.*

**Market Intelligence**

Every transaction generates pricing data, demand signals, and
operational insight that is not available through public sources. After
years of charter brokerage, OSA will understand what routes generate
consistent demand, which aircraft categories are underpriced relative to
their charter yield, and how pricing fluctuates seasonally. This
intelligence would cost millions to acquire through research --- it is
earned through transactions.

**Operator Relationships**

Part 135 operators are the backbone of the charter industry. They
control aircraft availability, set pricing floors, and often have first
knowledge of aircraft entering the market. Brokers who consistently
deliver quality clients to operators build relationships that translate
directly into preferential treatment: better availability, better
pricing, advance notice of aircraft for sale, and eventually partnership
opportunities.

When OSA eventually considers aircraft asset management or leasing,
these operator relationships become critical infrastructure. An operator
who has flown dozens of trips booked through OSA Connect is far more
likely to manage an OSA-owned aircraft than a stranger approaching with
capital.

**High-Net-Worth Client Exposure**

Private aviation clients spend meaningfully on charter --- often
\$100,000 to \$500,000 or more annually. These clients represent the
pool from which aircraft buyers emerge. A charter client who flies four
or five times per year is economically motivated to consider ownership
or fractional ownership.

OSA Connect is not just a charter brokerage --- it is a pipeline for
aircraft transaction clients. Every charter client relationship built is
a potential future OSA Aircraft engagement. This natural progression is
one of the most underappreciated advantages of the brokerage-first
model.

**Understanding of Charter Demand**

Aircraft owners frequently struggle to understand whether placing their
aircraft on charter is economically rational. OSA Connect's daily
visibility into charter demand --- what routes are busy, what cabin
sizes are in demand, what rates the market will bear --- is directly
applicable to advising aircraft owners on charter revenue strategies.
This knowledge is essential for aircraft asset management and,
eventually, for evaluating aircraft acquisition opportunities.

**Understanding of Aircraft Economics**

Aircraft brokerage exposes the founder to the full cost structure of
aircraft ownership: acquisition price, depreciation curves, maintenance
program enrollment, crew costs, insurance, hangar, and management fees.
This knowledge, developed through dozens of transactions and client
advisory engagements, is the foundation for credible aircraft asset
management and, eventually, for making sound capital allocation
decisions in aircraft leasing.

  **08**   **THE ROLE OF LOUD HAWK**

  -------- --------------------------------------------------------------

*A live media layer supporting the brokerage business. Ambition grows
with audience and credibility.*

Loud Hawk is now an active media operation, not a planned initiative.
The newsletter launched March 12, 2026. LinkedIn and Instagram channels
are live with content. A Content Strategy & Distribution Architecture
(v1.2) governs all content production as locked doctrine. AI-augmented
production skills handle content creation across channels with editorial
kill switches and quality scorecards built into the workflow.

This is evidence of operational readiness --- the ability to produce and
distribute content consistently. It is not evidence of product-market
fit, audience engagement, or inbound deal flow at scale. The first
inbound signal has occurred, which is encouraging but not proof of a
working acquisition channel. Loud Hawk must now demonstrate sustained
audience development and genuine industry engagement over quarters and
years, not days.

**What Systems Have Changed**

Content production infrastructure has significantly reduced the
bottleneck of content creation for a solo operator. The founder can now
produce LinkedIn posts, Instagram content, and newsletter issues at a
cadence that would have been impossible without AI-augmented workflows.
This means Loud Hawk can operate consistently alongside a full-time
airline career --- which was the primary constraint identified in v2.

**What Systems Have Not Changed**

Systems produce content. They do not produce trust, audience loyalty, or
industry respect. Those are earned through the quality and consistency
of output over time, through genuine industry participation, and through
the slow accumulation of readers and followers who engage because the
content is valuable --- not because it exists. A content production
system with no engaged audience is infrastructure, not a business.

**Loud Hawk Strategy --- Phase 1 (Current)**

-   Aviation newsletter, published on a consistent cadence, focused on
    aviation news, market commentary, and industry developments

-   Social media presence on LinkedIn and Instagram --- commentary on
    aviation news, thoughtful observations, industry analysis

-   Consistent, high-quality publishing builds more credibility than
    sporadic bursts. But quality controls override the calendar ---
    three strong posts always beat four mediocre ones

-   No paid content, complex data products, or sponsorship pursuit in
    early phases --- these will come naturally if the audience develops

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  *The goal of Loud Hawk in Phase 1 is not revenue. It is brand awareness
  and credibility. One inbound inquiry from a potential aircraft seller
  or charter client who found OSA through Loud Hawk content is worth more
  than any newsletter revenue in early years.*

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

**Loud Hawk as the Business Matures**

-   Deeper market analysis pieces: used aircraft market trends, charter
    demand analysis, aircraft category comparisons

-   Aviation industry reports published quarterly or annually ---
    positioning OSA as a market intelligence source

-   Data-driven content derived from OSA's own transaction experience
    (aggregated and anonymized)

-   Sponsorship revenue from aviation service providers: maintenance
    companies, avionics firms, charter operators

-   Potential paid intelligence product for aircraft owners, operators,
    or investors

The evolution from newsletter to intelligence platform should be
market-driven --- meaning Loud Hawk should develop what its audience
actually values, not what seems strategically interesting from the
outside. The audience will signal what they want if the founder pays
attention.

  **09**   **FOUNDER STRATEGY**

  -------- --------------------------------------------------------------

*Building OSA while working as an airline pilot. A realistic plan for a
founder starting from zero.*

The founder is a 32-year-old working First Officer on the Embraer E175
at SkyWest Airlines, holding an ATP with type rating and CFI/CFII/MEI
certificates. Dual citizen of Brazil and the United States, raised in
São José dos Campos --- Embraer's headquarters city. This background
provides both aviation credibility and a genuine cultural connection to
one of the world's most important aircraft manufacturers.

The founder's situation is genuinely advantageous, even though it may
not feel that way. Airline pilot income provides a financial base that
allows OSA to be built without desperate pressure to generate revenue
from day one. The aviation background provides credibility that most
aspiring aviation entrepreneurs lack. And the structured environment of
airline flying instills the discipline and attention to detail that
private aviation clients expect.

The challenge is time. Building any business part-time is slower than
building it full-time. The founder must be realistic about this and
resist the temptation to accelerate beyond what part-time execution
allows.

**The Part-Time Build Strategy**

-   Capital accumulation period began in 2025 --- the founder saves
    aggressively while studying the industry and building the brand

-   Loud Hawk launched during this period: leveraging AI-augmented
    production to maintain consistent output alongside airline
    responsibilities

-   First charter bookings are deliberately small and few --- the goal
    is learning the process, not volume

-   Aircraft brokerage begins only after genuine understanding of
    charter economics is developed --- not simultaneously from day one

-   The founder evaluates transition from airline pilot to full-time OSA
    when aviation income reliably exceeds 50% of airline income --- not
    before

**Systems as a Leverage Multiplier**

The AI-augmented production infrastructure and operating systems built
during the first year represent a meaningful leverage multiplier for a
solo, part-time operator. Content that would have required hours of
production time can now be generated, reviewed, and published in a
fraction of that time. Administrative workflows that would have consumed
evenings and days off are now systematized. This does not make OSA a
technology company --- it makes OSA a brokerage that uses technology to
compress the administrative burden of a part-time build.

The risk is mistaking this production leverage for business progress.
Systems make the founder more efficient. They do not make the founder
more credible, more connected, or more experienced. Those require
transactions, relationships, and time in the market.

  **11**   **REALISTIC END STATE**

  -------- --------------------------------------------------------------

*What OSA could realistically become after 20 years of disciplined
execution.*

The end state described here is realistic --- meaning it reflects what
is achievable with disciplined execution, favorable market conditions,
and the accumulation of genuine expertise over two decades. It is not a
guarantee. It is a plausible destination for a founder who executes the
preceding phases with consistency and patience.

**The Most Realistic End State: Aviation Advisory and Asset Management**

After 20 years, the most likely successful version of OSA is an aviation
advisory and asset management firm that manages aircraft assets on
behalf of individual and institutional owners, operates a recognized
charter brokerage, and publishes a respected aviation intelligence
product through Loud Hawk.

This version of OSA does not require owning a fleet of aircraft. It
generates fee income from the expertise and relationships accumulated
over two decades of brokerage and advisory work. Revenue in this
scenario could realistically reach \$5M--\$15M annually from combined
operations.

**A More Ambitious But Achievable End State: Aircraft Asset Platform**

If the founder successfully accumulates capital, develops institutional
relationships, and completes the educational path described above, OSA
could transition into an aircraft asset platform: managing a portfolio
of owned and managed aircraft, deploying capital selectively into
acquisition and leasing, and operating as a credible participant in
aviation finance.

This version of OSA manages \$50M--\$200M in aviation assets, generates
management fees and lease income on a recurring basis, and operates as a
recognized brand in the private aviation finance community. It is an
ambitious goal that requires everything to go reasonably well over a
long period --- which is not a given.

**What OSA Will Not Be**

It is equally important to be clear about what OSA is unlikely to become
in a 20-year window starting from zero: a large aircraft lessor
competing with GECAS or Air Lease Corporation; a private equity-style
aviation fund managing billions; or a technology platform disrupting the
charter market.

These outcomes require either significant outside capital, technology
infrastructure far beyond OSA's scope, or decades of scale that cannot
realistically be achieved from a standing start. Pursuing them at the
expense of the realistic end states described above is one of the most
common ways that promising aviation businesses fail.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  *The discipline to pursue a realistic long-term vision --- rather than
  an exciting but unachievable one --- is one of the most important
  strategic decisions OSA's founder can make. The aviation industry
  respects depth, longevity, and reliability. Those qualities are built
  over decades.*
