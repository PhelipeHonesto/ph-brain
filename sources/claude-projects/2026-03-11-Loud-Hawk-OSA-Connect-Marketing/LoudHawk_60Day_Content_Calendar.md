# LOUD HAWK — 60-Day Content Calendar

**Editions 1–4 | Weekly Operating Rhythm | Launch Through Review Gate**

---

## Operating Rhythm (Locked)

| Week type | Newsletter | LinkedIn | Instagram | Primary task |
|-----------|-----------|----------|-----------|-------------|
| Publish week | Full edition ships | Post: insight pulled from edition | Story: edition screenshot or key line | Ship + distribute |
| Off week | No send | Post: original observation (standalone) | Optional touch | Research + write next edition |

---

## House Rules (Locked)

1. **Off-week LinkedIn posts must be valuable even if nobody clicks anything.** No newsletter links in off-week posts. The insight stands alone. If someone finds your profile and subscribes, the system worked. If you have to direct them, the content wasn't strong enough.
2. **Every edition must pass the test: "Would I send this to a prospective OSA client as a credential?"** If the answer is no, it's not ready to publish.
3. **Every main claim must be internally tagged as one of:** directly observed, inferred from repeated observations, sourced from public reporting, or opinion/interpretation. This tag is for your internal use — it never appears in the newsletter. It keeps the writing honest about what you know versus what you're interpreting.

**Instagram (publish day)**

### Week 2: Off Week

**LinkedIn post**
- **Lane:** TG (The Gap)
- **Topic:** Pick one thing clients consistently get wrong about charter. Write it as a short, direct correction.
- **Angle examples** (choose one based on what's sharpest right now):
  - "Most people think charter pricing is fixed. It isn't. Here's what actually moves the number."
  - "The quote you got isn't the price. It's the starting position. Here's what's inside it that nobody explains."

## EDITION 2 — WEEK 3 & 4

### Week 3: Publish Week

**Edition 2**
- **Lane:** HIAW (How It Actually Works)
- **Main piece topic:** How charter operators actually price a trip.
- **Why this topic:** You noted operator behavior as available material. Pricing mechanics is the single most useful thing you can explain to a charter client, and almost nobody in the industry does it clearly. This is pure information-asymmetry reduction — exactly what Loud Hawk is for.
- **Main piece angle:** Walk through what goes into a charter quote — aircraft positioning, fuel, crew costs, landing fees, FET, broker markup structure. Not a cost breakdown spreadsheet. A clear explanation of why the same trip can be $22K on Tuesday and $34K on Thursday. Name the variables. Be specific.
- **Quick Hits:**
  - One Market Signal item (pricing shift or demand observation you've seen since Edition 1)
  - One Gap item (a short, sharp note about something the industry misrepresents — pull from your LinkedIn off-week post if it resonated)
- **The Close (attribution only — no service CTA):** "Loud Hawk is published by Orange Sunshine Aviation."
- **Subject line direction:** "What's actually inside your charter quote" or "Why the same trip costs $12K more on Thursday"

**LinkedIn post (publish day)**
- **Lane:** HIAW
- **Angle:** Pull one surprising variable from the pricing explanation. Example: the repositioning cost reality, or how FET works, or why "guaranteed quotes" have expiration logic most clients don't understand.
- **Structure:** Short, specific, educational. Open with something the reader assumes is simple and show them it isn't.
- **First comment:** Subscribe link.

**Instagram (publish day)**
- Story: Key line from Edition 2 as text overlay on a clean background. Link sticker.

## EDITION 3 — WEEK 5 & 6

### Week 5: Publish Week

**Edition 3**
- **Lane:** TG (The Gap)
- **Main piece topic:** What charter clients consistently get wrong — and why the industry benefits from keeping it that way.
- **Why this topic:** You flagged client misconceptions as available material. This is your strongest editorial lane on LinkedIn, and it's time to give it a full edition treatment. The Gap is also the most shareable content type per the Editorial Blueprint — it's the highest-signal trust builder.
- **Main piece angle:** Pick the 2–3 most common misconceptions you encounter. Don't write a listicle. Pick the one that costs clients the most money or creates the most risk, and build the piece around that. The others can be Quick Hits.
- **Possible misconceptions to build around:**
  - "The cheapest quote is the best deal" — what low quotes actually signal about operator quality, aircraft age, or hidden fees
  - "My broker works for me" — the actual incentive structure of most brokerage relationships
  - "I fly enough to justify a jet card / fractional" — when the math actually works and when it doesn't
- **Quick Hits:**
  - One Market Signal item (what you've observed in pricing or demand since Edition 2)
  - One Situational Awareness item if something timely has happened — FAA directive, notable incident, operator news. If nothing timely, use a second MS or HIAW item instead. Don't force SA content.
- **The Close (soft CTA):** "Need a second opinion on a quote? That's what we do. orangesunshineaviation.com"
- **Subject line direction:** Lead with the misconception. "Your broker's incentive isn't what you think" or "The cheapest charter quote is usually the most expensive mistake"

**LinkedIn post (publish day)**
- **Lane:** TG
- **Angle:** The single most provocative claim from the main piece. This is your most shareable edition — the LinkedIn post should be the sharpest version of the argument. If the main piece is about broker incentives, the LinkedIn post is "Most charter brokers don't work for you. Here's how the incentive actually works."
- **First comment:** Subscribe link.

**Instagram (publish day)**
- Feed post (not just a story): A text-based graphic with the core claim from the edition. Purple background, white text, short and punchy. This is the edition most likely to get shared on Instagram because it's contrarian and specific.

## 60-DAY REVIEW — WEEK 8

At this point you have published 4 editions and 8 LinkedIn posts. You have enough data to make real decisions.

### Review questions (from the operating rhythm):

**Performance:**
- What is the average open rate? (Target: 45%+. Niche B2B early-stage newsletters should be higher — 50–60% is realistic with a quality list.)
- Which edition had the highest open rate? Which had the most replies?
- Which LinkedIn posts generated the most profile visits and subscribe-page clicks?
- How many total subscribers? What's the week-over-week growth trend?

**Editorial:**
- Which lane produced the strongest content? (Tag review: how many MS, HIAW, TG, SA pieces total?)
- Which lane got the most engagement?
- Is there a lane you haven't used enough? Is SA still untapped because nothing timely came up, or because you're avoiding it?
- Are the editions getting easier to write, or harder?

**Operations:**
- Is a content backlog forming naturally from your daily work and observations?
- Is the biweekly cadence sustainable alongside your pilot schedule?
- Are you spending the off-weeks on research and writing, or are they getting consumed by other OSA tasks?

**Growth source:**
- Are subscribers coming primarily from your personal network, or are strangers finding Loud Hawk through LinkedIn and organic discovery?
- Have any subscribers forwarded an edition or mentioned it to someone?
- Has any OSA Connect inquiry ever referenced Loud Hawk? (This is the single strongest proof the system is working.)

**Important framing:** Subscriber count is a signal. Replies, forwards, and relationship quality are truth. Do not let the subscriber number become the scoreboard in your head. The Execution Memo is clear: Loud Hawk matters when it creates replies, relationships, and inbound relevance to OSA.

**Source-of-claim discipline:**
- Review your internal tags on each main piece. Were your claims mostly observed, inferred, sourced, or opinion?
- Is the balance shifting toward inference or opinion over time? If so, that's a warning sign — get back to direct observation.
- Did any claim feel shaky after publishing? Track those. Loud Hawk's credibility is built on honesty about what you know versus what you're interpreting.

### Decision gates:

**If the answers are strong:** Consider adding a short mid-cycle email ("Loud Hawk Signal" — 100–150 words, one market observation, no full structure). Test it for 2 editions before committing.

**If the answers are mixed:** Stay biweekly. Focus on improving the weakest area — content quality, distribution, or production sustainability.

**If the answers are weak:** Diagnose whether it's a content problem, an audience problem, or a distribution problem. Do not increase cadence. Consider whether the editorial lanes need adjustment.
