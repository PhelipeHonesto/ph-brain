**LOUD HAWK**

Editorial Blueprint

Version 1.0 \| March 2026

The editorial operating model for Loud Hawk --- the intelligence and
credibility layer of the Orange Sunshine Aviation ecosystem. This
document defines what Loud Hawk is, how it sounds, what it publishes,
and how every edition should be judged before it ships.

*This is a working document. Use it as a house style guide, an editorial
checklist, and a quality gate.*

**1. Publication Thesis**

> *Loud Hawk is private aviation intelligence for people who pay
> attention --- market signals, operator reality, and what's actually
> happening in the business of flying private.*

**2. Internal Editorial Mission**

Loud Hawk exists to build trust and informed attention at the top of the
OSA ecosystem. It attracts readers who are current or future private
aviation participants --- charter clients, aircraft owners, pilots,
operators, and market-curious professionals --- and earns their
credibility before they ever need a broker.

It does this by publishing sharp, specific observations about how the
private aviation market actually works. Every edition should make the
reader more informed, more skeptical of industry noise, and more
confident that the people behind Loud Hawk know what they're talking
about.

Loud Hawk is not a company blog. It is not a sales channel. It is not a
news aggregator. It is an editorial product that happens to be made by
the same people who run OSA Connect. The commercial value comes from
credibility, not from calls to action.

**3. Editorial Lanes**

Every piece of Loud Hawk content fits into one of four recurring lanes.
These lanes define the editorial territory and make content planning
repeatable.

  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------
  **Lane 1: Market   
  Signal**           

  **Covers**         Observable movements in private aviation: charter
                     pricing shifts, fleet availability, operator
                     expansion or contraction, seasonal demand patterns,
                     aircraft residual values, route-level dynamics.

  **Why it matters** The charter and aircraft markets are opaque by
                     design. Most clients have zero visibility into
                     what's normal, what's inflated, and what's changing.
                     Market Signal gives them that visibility.

  **Why it fits Loud This is the purest form of intelligence. It comes
  Hawk**             from working in the market daily and paying
                     attention to patterns that outsiders can't see.

  **Supports**       Credibility + Commercial Trust
  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------

  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------
  **Lane 2: How It   
  Actually Works**   

  **Covers**         The mechanics of private aviation that clients
                     experience but don't understand: how brokers source
                     aircraft, what Part 135 means, why repositioning
                     fees exist, how operators price trips, what happens
                     between the quote and wheels-up.

  **Why it matters** Information asymmetry in charter and aircraft
                     transactions is enormous. Most of the industry
                     profits from keeping it that way. This lane shrinks
                     the gap.

  **Why it fits Loud Explaining the machinery is the fastest way to prove
  Hawk**             you're not hiding behind it. Every edition makes
                     readers better-informed clients.

  **Supports**       Credibility + Commercial Trust
  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------

  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------
  **Lane 3: The      
  Gap**              

  **Covers**         The distance between how private aviation markets
                     itself and how it actually performs. Lifestyle
                     claims vs. operational reality. Guaranteed quotes
                     vs. what shows up on the ramp. Concierge promises
                     vs. actual service. Empty leg marketing vs. empty
                     leg truth.

  **Why it matters** The industry is full of marketing language that
                     obscures reality. The buyer who understands the gap
                     makes better decisions and trusts honest advisors
                     more.

  **Why it fits Loud This is where the editorial voice is sharpest.
  Hawk**             Saying what others won't is the highest-signal trust
                     builder and the most shareable content type.

  **Supports**       Attention + Credibility
  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------

  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------
  **Lane 4:          
  Situational        
  Awareness**        

  **Covers**         External events, regulatory changes, incidents, or
                     developments that affect how people fly private ---
                     not as breaking news, but with context. FAA
                     directives, notable accidents with client-relevant
                     lessons, FBO changes, insurance shifts,
                     international charter factors.

  **Why it matters** Readers encounter these events as headlines with no
                     explanation of what it means for them.
                     Contextualized curation is high-value, low-effort

  Hawk**             timely without requiring original analysis every

**4. Standard Issue Template**

Every edition of Loud Hawk follows this structure. Do not deviate in v1.
Consistency builds reader trust and makes production faster.

  ---------------- ---------- -------------------------------------------
  **Section**      **Word     **Purpose**
                   Count**    

  **The Signal**   50--75     Opening observation. One sharp, specific
                              statement --- a market observation, a
                              provocation, or a data point. No preamble,
                              no greeting, no throat-clearing. This is
                              why the reader opens the email.

  **The Main       400--500   One topic developed with real depth. Draws
  Piece**                     from Market Signal or How It Actually Works
                              most often, occasionally The Gap. Include
                              specifics, numbers when available, and a
                              clear interpretation. State your view.
                              Don't hedge.

  **Quick Hits**   150--250   Two or three shorter items at 50--80 words
                              each. Draw from remaining lanes, especially
                              Situational Awareness. A regulatory flag, a
                              market data point, an anonymized
                              observation from a recent deal. Keeps the
                              edition current and full.

  **The Close**    30--50     Quiet connection back to OSA. One sentence.
                              Rotate variations. No hard sell, no urgency
                              language. Just a reminder of who makes this
                              and how to reach them.
  ---------------- ---------- -------------------------------------------

**Total target per edition:** 650--875 words. Short enough to read in 4
minutes. Long enough to deliver substance.

**Publishing frequency:** Every two weeks. A biweekly cadence you can
sustain is worth more than a weekly cadence that collapses by edition 6.
Move to weekly only when content production is systematized.

**5. Writing Rules**

These govern how every piece of Loud Hawk content should sound. Apply
them to the newsletter, Instagram captions, and any editorial content
published under the Loud Hawk name.

1.  **Start with the sharpest thing you have to say.** No warm-ups, no
    greetings, no context-setting preamble. The first sentence should
    make the reader lean in.

2.  **Be specific or be quiet.** Name the aircraft type. Name the route.
    Name the price range. Vague observations are worthless in a
    publication that claims to provide intelligence.

3.  **State your view.** If you have an interpretation, say it directly.
    Hedging with "some might argue" or "it remains to be seen" weakens
    the voice. Say what you think and stand behind it.

4.  **No luxury language.** Never use: elevate, bespoke, unparalleled,
    world-class, premier, curated, seamless, exclusive access. These
    words are dead in Loud Hawk.

5.  **No pressure language.** Never use: act now, limited availability,
    don't miss out, secure your spot. Loud Hawk informs. It does not
    sell.

6.  **No empty authority claims.** Never say "as industry experts" or
    "with decades of experience." Let the content prove the expertise.
    If it doesn't, the claim won't save it.

7.  **Use aviation terminology when accurate.** Part 135, repositioning
    leg, ARG/US rating, FAR compliance --- these are fine. Jargon for
    jargon's sake is not. If a term doesn't add precision, cut it.

8.  **Write short sentences.** Loud Hawk's natural sentence length is
    8--18 words. Occasionally go longer for a complex idea, but the
    default is tight. If a sentence has two commas, it probably needs to
    be two sentences.

9.  **Never repost news without adding value.** If all you have is the
    headline, don't publish it. Loud Hawk's job is context and
    interpretation, not aggregation.

10. **Protect sources and client details.** Anonymize everything. Never
    name clients, specific deal terms, or operator relationships that
    could identify a transaction. The insight matters. The names don't.

11. **End clean.** No summary paragraphs. No "in conclusion." No
    restating the thesis. Make the final point and stop.

12. **Read it once before publishing and cut 10%.** Every edition should
    feel slightly tighter than comfortable. If nothing is left to cut,
    you're not being honest enough with yourself.

**6. CTA Philosophy**

Loud Hawk is editorially independent from OSA's commercial operation. It
is made by the same people and it exists inside the same ecosystem, but
it is not a sales funnel. The moment it reads like a broker newsletter,
it loses the credibility that makes it commercially valuable in the
first place.

**How OSA appears in Loud Hawk**

-   **Attribution, not promotion.** Every edition should acknowledge
    that Loud Hawk is made by OSA. This is a trust signal, not a sales
    pitch. A simple line in the footer or close is enough: "Loud Hawk is
    published by Orange Sunshine Aviation."

-   **One quiet CTA per edition, maximum.** The close of each edition
    can include a single, low-pressure line connecting back to OSA's
    services. Rotate between variations so it never feels formulaic.

-   **Never interrupt editorial content with a pitch.** The main piece
    and quick hits should contain zero commercial language. If a topic
    naturally relates to what OSA does, the reader will make the
    connection. You don't need to make it for them.

-   **Earn the conversion through content quality.** The commercial
    logic is simple: a reader who trusts Loud Hawk's intelligence will
    trust OSA's advice when they need a broker. The newsletter's job is
    to build that trust over months, not to convert on any single
    edition.

**CTA rotation examples**

*"Have a trip coming up? orangesunshineaviation.com"*

*"OSA advises on charter and aircraft decisions. If you have a question,
we'll give you a straight answer."*

*"Need a second opinion on a quote? That's what we do.
orangesunshineaviation.com"*

*"Loud Hawk is published by Orange Sunshine Aviation --- advisory-first
charter brokerage."*

**7. Pre-Publication Scorecard**

Run every edition through this checklist before publishing. If you
cannot check at least 7 of 8 boxes, the edition is not ready.

  ---- ------------------------------------------------------------------
  □    Does the opening line make the reader want to read the next
       sentence?

  □    Is the main piece specific enough that a competing broker could
       not have written it?

  □    Would the person writing the check for a \$40K charter flight find
       this useful, interesting, or worth knowing?

  □    Does every claim rely on something observable, specific, or
       experienced firsthand --- not on vague industry sentiment?

  □    Is the edition under 875 words?

  □    Is there zero luxury language, zero pressure language, and zero
       empty authority claims?

  □    Would you send this to a potential client without feeling like
       you're pitching them?

  □    If you cut 10% of the words, would the meaning survive?
  ---- ------------------------------------------------------------------

**Minimum passing score: 7 of 8.** If the edition fails the scorecard,
revise before publishing. A skipped edition is better than a weak one.

This blueprint governs Loud Hawk v1. Revisit and update after the first
10 editions, once you have enough data on what resonates, what's
sustainable, and what the audience is actually responding to. Until
then, follow it.
