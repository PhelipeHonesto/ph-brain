## ENTITY STRUCTURE RESEARCH

### The Decision: Where to Form the LLC

There are three realistic options. Each has trade-offs.

**Option A — Single Illinois LLC (Simplest)**

File one LLC in Illinois, where you live and will operate. One entity, one state, one set of filings. You're a charter broker, not holding aircraft — the asset protection argument for Wyoming is weaker when your primary asset is a brand and a laptop, not a $5M aircraft.

Cost: ~$150 filing + $75/year annual report. You may also need to publish a notice of formation depending on your county (Cook County requires this, costs ~$100-200 in a local newspaper).

Downside: Illinois has higher fees than Wyoming, requires member/manager disclosure in Articles of Organization, and imposes a replacement tax on pass-through entities (1.5% of net income for LLCs taxed as partnerships). Privacy is minimal.

**Option B — Wyoming LLC (Privacy + Low Cost)**

Wyoming offers anonymous ownership (no member names on public record), no state income tax, $100 filing fee, $60/year annual report. Strongest LLC statute in the country (first state to adopt LLC law, 1977). No information sharing with IRS.

However: if you're operating in Illinois (which you are — your clients, your home, your marketing all say Chicago), you must also register as a foreign LLC in Illinois. That means paying Illinois fees on top of Wyoming fees, plus maintaining a registered agent in both states. The privacy benefit is real but the cost and complexity doubles.

Cost: ~$100 WY filing + $60/yr WY annual + IL foreign registration (~$150) + IL annual report ($75/yr) + two registered agents.

Best for: founders who want ownership privacy, plan to scale nationally, or will hold significant assets in the LLC.

**Option C — Wyoming Holding LLC + Illinois Operating LLC (Maximum Protection)**

The "double LLC" structure: a Wyoming LLC acts as the parent/holding company (owns the brand, IP, and any future assets). An Illinois LLC is the operating company that conducts business, holds contracts, and takes on liability. The Illinois LLC is owned by the Wyoming LLC.

This is the structure sophisticated aviation businesses use for aircraft ownership (aircraft in one LLC, operations in another). For a charter broker, it's arguably over-engineered at pre-revenue stage — but it positions you correctly for OSA Aircraft down the road.

Cost: ~$100 WY + $150 IL + both annual fees + two registered agents. More accounting complexity (inter-company transactions).

Best for: founders building a multi-entity aviation platform with long-term asset protection goals. Your 20-year roadmap actually supports this structure.

### Recommendation

**Start with Option A (single Illinois LLC) today.** Get operational. You can always convert to Option B or C later — it's a reorganization, not a rebuild. The $950/hr attorney time is better spent on contracts than on entity restructuring at zero revenue.

If you're strongly drawn to Wyoming privacy and your attorney agrees, Option B is reasonable. Option C is the correct long-term architecture for OSA as a platform but is premature today.

## DOT / FAA REGULATORY REQUIREMENTS

### 14 CFR Part 295 — Air Charter Brokers

This is the governing federal regulation for charter brokers, effective February 2019. Key facts:

**No license required.** Part 295 does not require a broker to be licensed, certified, or pass any exam. The barrier to entry is intentionally low. However, compliance obligations are real.

**DOT Registration.** Brokers must register with the DOT Office of Aviation Analysis. Registration includes legal/trade names, business address, and names of owners and key personnel. There is no government-sponsored registry — only voluntary third-party registries exist.

**Mandatory Disclosures (before contract).** Every booking requires disclosure to the client of:
1. The corporate name of the direct air carrier operating the flight (and any DBAs)
2. The capacity in which the broker is acting (indirect air carrier, agent of charterer, or agent of carrier)
3. Whether the broker holds liability insurance

**Disclosures upon request:**
4. Any relationship between broker and carrier affecting carrier selection
5. Total cost including taxes and fees
6. Refund and cancellation policies

**Advertising rules.** All marketing materials (including website) must clearly state that OSA Connect is an air charter broker, not a direct air carrier, and that flights are operated by licensed Part 135 carriers.

**What this means for OSA Connect:** Your website, contracts, email signatures, and all client communications must include the Part 295 disclosures. Your Operating System already has most of this built into the SOP suite — but the website needs a compliance pass before you take a booking.

## LAUNCH CHECKLIST

### Phase 1 — This Week (Entity + Banking)

- [ ] **Decide entity structure** — Illinois LLC recommended (see research above)
- [ ] **File LLC** — Illinois Secretary of State, online, ~$150. Processing: 5-10 business days (expedited available)
- [ ] **Publish notice of formation** — Required in Cook County. Find a low-cost legal newspaper (~$100-200)
- [ ] **Apply for EIN** — IRS.gov, free, 15 minutes online. Requires LLC to be filed first
- [ ] **Open business bank account** — Chase, Mercury, or Relay. Requires EIN + Articles of Organization
- [ ] **Register domain for OSA entity** — if osaconnect.com or orangesunshineaviation.com not already secured

### Phase 2 — This Month (Insurance + Registration)

- [ ] **E&O Insurance (Errors & Omissions)** — Contact your insurance friend THIS WEEK. This is the critical coverage for a charter broker — protects you if a trip goes wrong due to broker error. Get quotes from aviation-specialist insurers
- [ ] **General Liability Insurance** — Standard business liability. Can often bundle with E&O
- [ ] **DOT Registration** — Register with DOT Office of Aviation Analysis as an air charter broker under Part 295. No fee. Requires LLC to exist first
- [ ] **Website compliance pass** — Add Part 295 required disclosures: "OSA Connect is an air charter broker and is not a direct air carrier. All flights are operated by FAA-licensed Part 135 carriers."

### Phase 3 — Before First Booking (Contracts + Compliance)

- [ ] **Charter Brokerage Agreement** — Client-facing contract. Must include Part 295 disclosures. Your $950/hr attorney should draft this. Ask for: (a) client agreement template, (b) operator agreement template, (c) non-circumvention clause
- [ ] **Operator Agreement** — Terms between you and the Part 135 operator for each trip. Your Operating System doc has the framework — attorney needs to formalize it
- [ ] **Non-Circumvention Agreement** — Already locked as Decision 6 in your CRM. Needs legal language
- [ ] **FET (Federal Excise Tax) understanding** — 7.5% FET applies to air transportation. Understand who collects and remits (typically the operator, but broker needs to know the flow)
- [ ] **WYVERN or ARGUS consideration** — Not required, but WYVERN Wingman Broker ($TBD) or ARGUS Broker membership gives you access to safety data and operator vetting tools. Strengthens credibility. Research pricing — this may be a Phase 2/3 decision

### Phase 4 — Ongoing Compliance

- [ ] **Keep DOT registration current** — Update after any ownership changes or business restructuring
- [ ] **Maintain disclosure records** — Every booking: document what was disclosed to client, when
- [ ] **Insurance renewal tracking** — Calendar E&O and GL renewal dates
- [ ] **Annual LLC filings** — Illinois annual report ($75/yr)
