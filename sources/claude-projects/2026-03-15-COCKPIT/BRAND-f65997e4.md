# Loud Hawk — Brand & Voice Reference

**Publisher:** Orange Sunshine Aviation
**Author:** Phelipe Honesto
**Format:** Biweekly newsletter (Beehiiv)
**Launched:** March 2026

---

## What Loud Hawk Is

Private aviation intelligence for charter brokers, HNW clients, and aviation insiders. Each edition reveals the operational layer that clients never see — the filtering, judgment, compliance work, and expertise behind a trip. The newsletter's signature lens: *the client sees X; the broker built Y.*

Loud Hawk is not a trade publication and not a luxury lifestyle newsletter. It sits between them: rigorous enough to earn broker respect, accessible enough to educate a sophisticated but non-expert client. Both audiences read the same issue and get something meaningful from it.

---

## Audience

**Primary:** High-net-worth charter clients — intelligent, time-constrained, skeptical of sales language. They've chartered before. They don't know what they don't know about how the industry works.

**Secondary:** Charter brokers and aviation operators — the people who do the work the newsletter describes. They read to feel seen, validated, and positioned to share the content with their clients.

**Tertiary:** Aviation insiders — industry professionals who follow the market.

---

## Voice

**Authoritative.** Loud Hawk knows this industry. Assertions are stated, not hedged. "A broker who skips past them is optimizing for speed, not for the client." Not "some brokers may not fully consider."

**Direct.** No filler. No wind-up. Lead with the point.

**Insider, not exclusive.** The tone assumes the reader is intelligent, not that they already know everything. Insider knowledge is explained, not assumed — but without condescension. The reader learns something they didn't know, and the explanation respects their sophistication.

**Unsentimental about money.** Private aviation is expensive. The newsletter doesn't celebrate or apologize for that. It focuses on decision quality.

**Independent.** No advertising voice, no operator endorsements, no sponsored-content register. Loud Hawk's credibility depends entirely on being willing to say the thing that isn't convenient.

---

## Tone Calibration

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| State the insight directly | Bury the lead in context-setting |
| Use specific examples (airports, routes, aircraft types) | Stay abstract when a concrete example exists |
| Name the thing the client doesn't know | Assume they already know it |
| Recommend against your own interest when the mission calls for it | Always push the premium option |
| Write short sentences that land | Write long compound sentences that dilute impact |
| Let silence do work — not every paragraph needs a transition | Over-explain or over-connect |

---

## Prohibited

- Superlatives without evidence ("the best," "the most important")
- Passive voice when active is possible
- Lifestyle language ("seamless," "effortless," "elevated experience")
- Hedging under pressure ("it's worth noting that," "some might argue")
- Filler transitions ("In today's edition," "Now let's talk about")
- Exclamation points

---

## Sentence Style

Short to medium. One idea per sentence under pressure. Vary rhythm: a long analytical sentence followed by a short declarative one. The short sentence carries the weight.

**Example from Edition 1:**
> "The client chose an aircraft. The broker chose the decision set."

This is the register. The second sentence reframes the first. That gap — between what the client thinks happened and what actually happened — is where Loud Hawk lives.

---

## Structure

Every edition follows the same two-section format:

### THE SIGNAL
The main feature. One central insight, developed rigorously. ~800–1,000 words. Not a listicle. A single argument, built in stages, with a concrete example as the anchor and a specific, actionable close (usually one question the reader can take into their next broker conversation).

### QUICK HITS
Three short items, each ~75–100 words. One insight. One takeaway. No filler. Each should be independently useful — a reader who skips THE SIGNAL should still get value from QUICK HITS.

### FOOTER
> Loud Hawk is published biweekly by Orange Sunshine Aviation. Questions about charter or the market? Reply here.

---

## Editorial Principles

1. **Reveal, don't sell.** Every edition reveals something the client didn't know was happening on their behalf. The newsletter earns trust by making the reader smarter, not by making private aviation sound glamorous.

2. **Name the failure mode.** The most useful insight is often what goes wrong when the work isn't done. Name it specifically. "A broker who accepts a 12-hour booking on a transatlantic trip without flagging permit timelines is skipping a step."

3. **One example, done well.** Each feature should have one vivid, specific operational example that carries the abstract concepts. Aspen (Edition 1). Miami → São Paulo (Edition 2). The example is not decoration — it is the vehicle.

4. **The question close.** Every feature ends with one concrete question the reader can ask their broker. This is the action bridge between intelligence and behavior change.

5. **No filler Quick Hits.** If a Quick Hit doesn't deliver a standalone insight and actionable takeaway in 75–100 words, it isn't a Quick Hit — it's a footnote. Cut it or expand it.

---

## What Loud Hawk Is Not

- Not a market report (no pricing data, no fleet statistics as the main event)
- Not a buyer's guide (no operator recommendations, no aircraft comparisons for purchase)
- Not a trade gossip column
- Not a sponsored content vehicle
- Not a lifestyle publication
