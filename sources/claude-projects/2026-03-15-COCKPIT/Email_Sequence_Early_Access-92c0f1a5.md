## Email 2: The Problem We Solve

```
Send:     Day 2
Subject:  What happens after you call a charter broker
Preview:  The callback loop, the PDF quote, and the fee that wasn't in it.
```

[First Name],

Here's the standard charter booking process:

You call a broker. They say they'll have something for you shortly. They contact three or four Part 135 operators, each of whom checks fleet availability and repositioning logistics. By mid-afternoon (sometimes by the following morning) you have a PDF.

The PDF says $18,500.

It doesn't mention the repositioning fee, because the aircraft isn't actually based at your departure airport. It doesn't break out the fuel surcharge, which moves with jet-A prices and was recalculated the day the quote was issued. The overnight handling at your destination FBO isn't in the number.

You say yes. The invoice arrives. It's $23,400.

This isn't fraud. Most of it is disclosed somewhere: in the charter agreement, in the fine print appended to the quote. But the number you made your decision on wasn't the number you paid. And by the time you saw the invoice, the trip had already happened.

OSA Connect quotes all-in from the start. Charter rate, fuel, fees, and applicable taxes in one number. That number is what appears on your invoice.

We also confirm operator availability before we issue a price. We don't quote an aircraft we haven't confirmed can fly your trip.

Neither of these is standard practice in this industry.

Both of them should be.

Phelipe Honesto
Founder, OSA Connect

```
CTA:       None. Closes on a position statement.
Length:    ~220 words
Tone note: The numbered fee breakdown is the specific operational example the brand
           requires. "This isn't fraud" does heavy lifting: honest, disarming, and
           sets OSA apart without attacking competitors by name.
```

## Email 4: The Founder Story

```
Send:     Day 8
Subject:  From São Paulo to a Part 121 flight deck
Preview:  Dual citizenship, Embraer, and a route that shapes how I think about charter.
```

[First Name],

I grew up in Brazil. I hold dual citizenship: Brazilian and American. I moved to the United States through aviation and now fly an Embraer ERJ-175 for a living, an aircraft designed and built in São José dos Campos, Brazil.

That's not coincidence. It's context.

The Miami–São Paulo corridor is one of the most operationally complex charter routes in the Western Hemisphere. On the US side: FAA Part 135 certification, crew rest requirements, departure and overflight planning through Central American airspace. On the Brazilian side: ANAC certification, Brazilian airspace filing, customs at Guarulhos, and handler coordination that most US operators have never managed directly.

Most charter brokers who quote that route hand it to a local handling agent and wait. I've flown that corridor commercially. I understand both sides of that clearance in both languages: the regulatory requirements and the operational realities.

OSA Connect isn't built exclusively for international trips. Most of what we'll book will be domestic point-to-point, light to midsize jets, straightforward missions.

But the same foundation applies whether the destination is Nashville or Guarulhos: understand what you don't know and build the knowledge to fill it.

That's the background I'm working from. Next time, I'll explain what I want from you before we launch.

Phelipe Honesto
Founder, OSA Connect

## Metrics Plan

**Open rate benchmark:** 35–45% (pre-launch list, high-intent subscribers)
**Click rate benchmark:** 8–15% on Email 3 (only email with an external link)
**Reply rate benchmark:** 3–8% on Email 5. This is the primary conversion metric.

**What to track:**
- Email 3 click-through to Loud Hawk Edition 1 (validates content credibility)
- Email 5 reply rate by day (test: send Tuesday vs. Thursday, both 9 a.m. local)
- Unsubscribe rate after Email 2 (if high, the problem framing is too broad or off-audience)

**Segment to watch:** Subscribers who open all 5 but don't reply to Email 5 are warm prospects without a trip ready. Re-engage at launch with a direct "we're live" email that mirrors Email 5's copy.

## Production Notes

Send as plain text in Beehiiv. No header image, no HTML template. The plain-text format is intentional. The product at this stage is the founder's direct attention. A designed template signals a marketing operation; a plain email signals a person.

The address `fly@osaconnect.com` should appear as visible text in Emails 1, 4, and 5, not only in the From field, so subscribers can reply even if their client doesn't thread correctly.
