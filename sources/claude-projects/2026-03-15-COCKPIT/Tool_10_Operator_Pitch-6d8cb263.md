# Tool 10 — Operator Pitch

**Purpose:** Open a working relationship with a Part 135 operator before you have deal flow to show them. This is not a sales pitch in the conventional sense — it is a credibility document. The goal is not to convince a skeptical operator to take a chance on you. The goal is to give a reasonable operator enough information to decide whether you are worth a 20-minute call.

**When to use it:** First contact with any operator you have not worked with before. Send it as a PDF attachment to an email introduction, or bring a printed copy to an in-person meeting (NBAA, fly-in, FBO visit). Do not paste it into the body of an email — that reads as a form letter.

**What operators actually fear from new brokers:** Wasted availability holds, clients who don't close, bad trip specs that cause operational problems, and brokers who disappear after one inquiry. Your pitch addresses each of these directly. It does not hide that you are early-stage. Operators can detect that. What you can show them is that you are worth betting on early.

**Who I Am**

My name is Phelipe Honesto. I am a First Officer at SkyWest Airlines flying the Embraer E175 — ATP with a type rating, CFI/CFII/MEI certificates. I grew up in São José dos Campos, Brazil, a city built by Embraer. I have spent my career in aviation operations, not on the periphery of it.

I am building OSA Connect, a charter brokerage. I am at the beginning of that build. I am telling you that upfront, because a broker who hides it is a broker who will waste your time in other ways too.

---

**What I Am Building — and Why That Matters to You**

Most new brokers call operators with incomplete trip specs, unqualified clients, and no process. I am not building that. Before I contacted a single operator, I built the following:

- A client qualification system. I do not send you a quote request until I know the client has decision authority, firm dates, and is ready to commit. Tire-kickers do not make it to your inbox.
- A complete trip spec on every inquiry — routing, PAX count, luggage, catering requirements, departure window, flexibility, and client background — delivered before I ask for availability.
- An operator vetting protocol. You are not the third call I make on every trip. You are one of two or three operators I contact when the trip fits your aircraft and your base. I do not blast quotes to a list.
- A post-trip follow-up process. I debrief every trip with the client and report back to the operator. Good performance gets documented. Problems get addressed directly.

These are not promises. They are the system I built before I had trips to run through it. You will see the difference on the first inquiry.

---

**What You Get From Working With Me Early**

Operators who work with me now get something that operators I approach in Year 3 will not: full attention on every trip. I am not managing a pipeline of 40 active clients. When I bring you a trip, it is the trip I am focused on that week. You will have my cell number and you will reach me.

I am also building Loud Hawk, a private aviation intelligence newsletter for HNW charter clients and aviation insiders. Content covers the operational layer of charter brokerage — permit infrastructure, operator vetting, how aircraft are sourced, what clients don't know about the trips they're booking. Operators who work with me are part of the story I am telling the market about what a professional brokerage relationship looks like. I do not name operators without their consent. But a client base that understands what good operations look like is a client base worth having.

## USAGE NOTES

**The covering email.** Do not send the pitch without a short personal note. The note does the work the pitch cannot: it shows you did basic research on this operator and you have a specific reason for reaching out to them. Template:

> Subject: Charter broker introduction — OSA Connect
>
> [Name] —
>
> My name is Phelipe Honesto. I'm building OSA Connect, a charter brokerage based on [region / route context relevant to this operator]. I'm an E175 First Officer at SkyWest with an ATP, and I'm building this with the operational standards I'd want if the positions were reversed.
>
> I'm not going to pretend I have a book of business to offer you today. What I have is a process I built before I made my first operator call, and a clear picture of the kind of broker I intend to be. I attached a one-pager.
>
> If you're open to a 20-minute call, I'd like to understand your preferred aircraft, base, and the kinds of trips you're best positioned for. I'm not fishing for quotes — I'm building a short list of operators I can put real clients in front of.
>
> [Your name]
> [Phone]

**On honesty about your stage.** Some founders hide that they're early. Do not do this. Operators talk to each other. If you misrepresent your pipeline and it comes out — and it will — you lose the relationship and the referral network that comes with it. Honesty about your stage, paired with genuine competence signals (ATP, professional process), lands better than overclaiming. The operator's actual question is not "does this broker have clients today?" It is "is this broker someone I would trust with a client if they got one?" Answer that question.

**On the ATP credential.** Lead with it, but do not over-explain it. You do not need to say "this means I understand aviation operations." Operators know what it means. State it once and let it sit. The operators who find it meaningful will find it. The ones who don't will not be moved by a longer explanation.

**On the Loud Hawk mention.** Include it. An operator who reads Loud Hawk Edition 1 or 2 before you call them will approach the conversation differently than one who hasn't. If you have a specific edition that is relevant to their operation — international routing, seasonal demand patterns — mention it in the covering email: "I covered international permit infrastructure in my last issue — it's the kind of thing I think about when I'm building trip specs for operators doing that routing."

**Track every operator contact in your CRM.** Date of first contact, how you reached them, what you learned about their preferred trips and base, named dispatcher or scheduling contact, outcome of the call, follow-up date. This is your most valuable long-term asset. Operators who do not respond now may respond in Year 2 when you have a track record. Do not lose the thread.

---

*Tool 10 is a living document. After your first 10 operator conversations, revise the pitch based on the objections you actually heard — not the ones you predicted. The pitch that wins conversations is always more specific than the one you write before the conversations happen.*

---

**A note on where this belongs in the sequence:** Run Tool 3 (Operator Vetting) on an operator before you use Tool 10. You should know their aircraft, their base, their safety rating, and whether their typical trips match your client profile before you make contact. An introduction that demonstrates you already understand their operation is categorically different from a cold pitch. That specificity is what separates you from the broker who blasts the same email to a list of 40 operators.
